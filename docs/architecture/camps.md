# Camps, heroes and resources

The keys of the two tables and the transactions that write them: the camp, its facilities and holdings, heroes, the ledger, the heroes seen at a location, and the location's recent adventures and replays. The game's side is `docs/design/game.md`, "Locations and camps", "The heroes hall" and "Resources"; the key shapes and their reasons are decisions 0026, 0029, 0050 and 0053. Why there are two tables is in `docs/architecture/overview.md`, "Services and data"; this was a section of that document until Issue-282.

Each item carries a status: **Decided**, **Working assumption** or **Open**, as `docs/design/game.md` defines them. An item marked **Built** names the issue that built it.

## The keys and what writes them

- **Decided.** The player table's keys, all in the player's one item collection, so the camp loads with one Query of `PLAYER#<id>`:

  | Sort key | Item |
  | --- | --- |
  | `PROFILE` | the player |
  | `AT#<location>#CAMP` | the camp: hall and warehouse levels, the counts of active and of living heroes, kept by every transaction that changes them (recruit and retire so far); no item means the starting location at level 1 with no heroes |
  | `AT#<location>#FACILITY#<kind>` | an own facility the camp builds and its level, absent until built; the hall's and the warehouse's levels are on the camp's item (decision 0032) |
  | `AT#<location>#HOLDING#<resource>` | a resource balance at that location |
  | `HERO#<heroId>` | a hero, with its location as an attribute, and the open adventure's id while it is on it |
  | `LEDGER#<causeId>#<entry>` | append-only; the cause is an adventure id, a hero id or a build, `build-<location>-<kind>-<level>`, and the entry can exist once |
  | `OPEN` | the one open adventure |

- **Decided.** A hero's item: unit type, sprite set and name, chosen by the hub at recruitment and never by the client; location; recruited at; level, XP and the stat-boost picks (the choices, not the resulting numbers, so they survive a change to the stat tables); weapon and its fights left; status, alive, dead or retired, and when it retired; the open adventure it is on, set at start and cleared at settlement, while it is on one; history counters. Fields a later feature adds default in code.
- **Decided.** Recruiting is one mutation, `recruitHero(unitType, requestId)`, and one transaction: the camp's active count below the hall's cap, then incremented; one conditional debit and one ledger entry per resource in the cost, or none for a villager when the camp's count of living heroes is zero, which the condition checks; the hero item put if absent. The request id becomes the hero id, so a repeated request fails its condition and returns the hero already made. The hub draws the sprite set and then a name that suits its label (masculine, feminine or either; game.md, "Heroes") from the static content with a cryptographic random source, avoiding names on the camp's living roster.
- **Decided.** The profile is made on first sign-in by `joinGame`, which the client calls after every sign-in: one update that writes the profile if absent and leaves it alone if present. Nothing else creates a profile. A Cognito trigger is not used: every sandbox stack shares one user pool (decision 0022), so a trigger cannot know which stack's table to write, and the trigger after sign-up fires once per user, not once per stack.
- **Built (Issue-81).** The keys above for the camp, the hero, the holdings and the ledger; `joinGame`; `recruitHero` for the free villager, with the cap and the free-villager rule as conditions on the camp's counts, proven by data-layer tests under concurrent recruits and repeated requests; the player's camps and heroes on `me`, owner-only; the static content of Millbrook, the first location, in `packages/engine/src/content.ts`. The stack test recruits the free villager and checks that a second is refused. The client (Issue-81, second pull request): the header bar with the location select and the open adventure; `joinGame` after every sign-in; the camp scene in PixiJS (decision 0028) with the ground, the hall, the job board and the warehouse, and the living heroes walking about; the hall's roster and the free villager, who walks out of the hall; a hero's card on a click. The page's regions (Issue-89, `docs/design/game.md`, "The page"): the facility menu with its icons, the persistent content area with the panel as a child route of the camp (`/camp/<location>/<facility>`, `/camp/<location>/heroes-hall/heroes/<id>`, `packages/client/src/app/app.routes.ts`), each panel its own component under `camp/panels/`, the narrow-screen order, and the scene filling its box around Millbrook's 400×225 map, in `packages/client/src/app/camp/`, with the arithmetic in `page-layout.ts`. Millbrook lists a mine, a lumber camp and a caravan hub as `buildableFacilities` in the engine's content, each with its description in `FACILITIES`; they stand at level 0 until built (Issue-93).
- **Built (Issue-98).** Retiring is one mutation, `retireHero(heroId)`, and one transaction, `packages/hub/src/retire.ts`: the hero's status set to retired, with the time, on the condition that it is still the status read, alive or dead; the camp's active count decremented, and its living count too if the hero was alive, on the condition that neither goes below zero. A concurrent retire, death or move fails the hero's condition, so the counts change once; a recruit that read the camp before a retire fails its own condition on the counts. A hero already retired is answered as it is. Retiring the only living hero makes the next villager free. A hero on the open adventure cannot retire (Issue-113). Proven by data-layer tests under concurrent retires and a retire racing a recruit for the last slot; the stack test retires its villager and recruits the free villager again on a sandbox stack. The client: a Retire button on an active hero's card, behind a confirmation in place; a retired hero's card says when it retired and offers nothing; the hall's roster lists the retired apart, collapsed; and a retired hero walks back into the hall and leaves the scene.
- **Built (Issue-82).** Paid recruitment and the roster. `recruitHero` recruits any unit type on the location's list at its hall level, `Repository.recruit` in `packages/hub/src/db/repository.ts`: one transaction puts the hero, counts it into the camp on the condition that the hall is at the level read and below its cap, debits each resource of the cost on the condition that the balance covers it with a ledger entry `LEDGER#<heroId>#debit#<material>` that can exist once, and puts the sighting. The price is one rule in the engine, `recruitCost`, which the hub charges and the hall shows: a villager is free when the camp has no living hero and costs its price otherwise, and only the villager's transaction holds the living count, zero for a free one and above zero for a paid one, so a death or a recruit between the read and the write cannot change what was charged. Refusals, as data (decision 0038): `HallLevel`, `HallFull`, `InsufficientResources` with `short` (each material's amount and the camp's balance), and `Conflict` only when a lost race is explained by no rule; a request that loses a race reads the camp again and is refused with the rule that now fails; a unit type the location does not recruit is `BAD_USER_INPUT`. Proven by data-layer tests of two recruits for the last slot, two for the timber of one, a paid recruit racing a retire for the last slot, a repeated paid request, and a paid fighter with ore put into the holdings directly, since no job yields ore until Issue-133. The stack test asks for a fighter and is refused for want of ore, and after the timber run recruits a paid villager for 20 timber and retires it, on every stack. The client: the hall's roster grouped living, dead and retired, the retired collapsed; every recruit the location offers, its price beside the camp's balance, and why it cannot be had; the hub's refusal shown in place on the recruit it refused.
- **Built (Issue-99).** Heroes in the camp scene are drawn at half the scene's scale, rounded up (`heroScale` in `packages/client/src/app/camp/page-layout.ts`; decision 0028, as amended by Issue-99): the canvas renders at the scene's scale, each hero on a whole canvas pixel, walking at its own art pixels' pace.
- **Working assumption.** A caravan with passengers takes a slot at the destination in the start transaction, conditional on a free slot there, and settlement releases one of the two slots by the outcome. A party is validated at start from the camp's living heroes.
- **Built (Issue-93, decision 0032).** Building and upgrading a facility is one mutation, `buildFacility`, its input naming the camp, the facility and the level being built, so a repeat finds it built and is answered with the camp, charging nothing (`packages/hub/src/build.ts`). One transaction, `Repository.build`: the facility's level set to the next on the condition that it is still the level read, each facility the level needs checked at its level or above, and each resource of the cost debited on the condition that the balance covers it, with a ledger entry `LEDGER#build-<location>-<kind>-<level>#debit#<material>` that can exist once. The hall's and the warehouse's levels are on the camp's item, which the recruit and settle transactions already condition on; every other own facility is its own item, `AT#<location>#FACILITY#<kind>`, absent until built. The costs, levels and prerequisites are `FACILITIES` in the engine's content, and the reason a level cannot be built is one engine rule, `buildRefusal`, which the hub checks and the panel shows. Refusals, as data: `MaxLevel`, `Requires` with `missing`, `InsufficientResources` with `short`, `Conflict`; no such camp or facility, or a level that is not the next, is `BAD_USER_INPUT`. The camp answers each facility's level on `Camp.facilities`. Proven by data-layer tests of five concurrent builds of one level, two builds for the timber of one, a stale level, a stale balance, a hall upgrade racing a recruit and a requirement held in the transaction. The stack test builds the mine once its test user's camp can pay, and checks that a repeat charges nothing and that the mine has no second level. The client: a build section in each own facility's panel and in the hall's, and the scene swapping a facility's picture when its level changes.
- **Built (Issue-133).** A job names the facility that posts it, `facility` in the engine's `Job`, or none for the job board; `jobsPostedBy(location, poster)` is the one rule for which jobs a poster lists, which the panels show. The first is the mine's "Ore from the hill", which pays for a fighter (decision 0031). `startAdventure` finds the job anywhere at the location and refuses a facility's job at a camp that has not built the facility with `Requires`, the facility in `missing`, as building does; the start's transaction checks the facility's item, `AT#<location>#FACILITY#<kind>`, at level 1 or above, so a start that read the facility built and finds no item is refused when it reads again. `Job.facility` says the poster on the graph. Proven by data-layer tests of the refusal, of the start once the mine is built, and of the condition held in the transaction against a camp read with the mine; the engine's test replays the job over 500 seeds with a lone villager and with a fighter and a villager. The client: the mine's panel lists its jobs once built, a job's detail sits under its poster, `/camp/<location>/mine/jobs/<id>`, and the job board lists only its own. The stack test is refused the job while its camp has no mine, and once it has one plays it, recruits a fighter for the ore and retires it.
- **Working assumption.** A credit past the warehouse cap stops at the cap. DynamoDB cannot take a minimum inside an update, so settlement reads the balances, computes the capped credits and writes them on the condition that each balance is unchanged, retrying on a conflict; the ledger records the amount lost.
- **Decided (decision 0030).** What settlement writes besides the credits, all in the one transaction: the job's XP to each surviving hero, with its level from a threshold table in the static content, and no stat-boost picks yet (game.md, "Heroes", keeps the picks; they come with a later issue); a hero at zero health when the adventure ends is dead, so its status becomes dead, the camp's living count is decremented on the condition that it is above zero, and its sighting is deleted; each survivor's sighting is refreshed (decision 0029). The result the response carries says per stage how it ended, and overall the rewards, the amount lost at the cap, the XP and the dead.
- **Decided.** The heroes of other players shown in a camp are a bounded sample, read from the location table (decision 0029):

  | PK | SK | Item |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `LOCATION#<location>` | `SEEN#<heroId>` | a hero seen there: name, unit type, sprite set and level, never its owner; `expiresAt`, the table's TTL attribute, a week after it was seen |

  A hero is seen when it does something: recruiting puts its sighting and retiring deletes it, each in the hero's own transaction; settlement refreshes a survivor's and deletes a dead hero's (Issue-114). Opening or switching camps is not a sighting. `heroesSeenAt(location)` reads at most 20 items from a random point among the sightings, wrapping round once, drops the expired, which DynamoDB deletes only within days, and the caller's own heroes, and returns up to 6 at random.
- **Built (Issue-100).** The sighting in the recruit and retire transactions, the TTL on the location table, and `heroesSeenAt` in `packages/hub/src/seen.ts`, proven by data-layer tests of the bound, the expiry and the exclusion of the caller's heroes. The client reads the sample when the player signs in or changes camps, not after each recruit, walks it about the scene beside the player's own heroes, and opens one on a read-only card, `/camp/<location>/seen/<id>`, with its name, unit type and level.
- **Decided (decision 0050).** The adventures recently completed at a location, built by Issue-288, and the replays of a job, built by Issue-289, both in the location table:

  | Key | Item |
  | --- | --- |
  | `LOCATION#<location>` / `DONE#<settledAt>#<adventureId>` | an adventure completed there: the job, the outcome, each stage's ending, what was credited and the party's public fields, never its owner; `expiresAt` a week after it settled |
  | `GSI1`: `GSI1PK = JOB#<jobId>`, `GSI1SK = <settledAt>` | the adventure record, from settlement on; an open or discarded record carries neither attribute and is not in the index |

  The settle transaction puts the feed item and sets the record's index keys; a discard writes neither. `recentAdventuresAt(location)` reads the newest 20 of the location's `DONE#` items and drops the expired (Issue-288); `replaysOf(job)` reads the newest 20 of the job's records from the index, which projects every attribute and is eventually consistent (Issue-289). Both need a signed-in player and serve public fields only: the feed item stores nothing else, and the record keeps its player's id, which no field serves (decision 0043). The client lists them on the job board's panel and on a job's page, and plays any settled adventure's timeline on a page of its own (Issue-290). Nothing settled before the deploy is backfilled into either. A player's own history is not served by the index; it is designed with its feature, in the player table. The prefixes `FACILITY#` and `CONTRIB#` in the location's collection are kept for step 5's shared facilities, which are not designed.
- **Built (Issue-288).** The feed item and its read, in `packages/hub/src/recent.ts`. `Repository.settle` puts the item beside its other writes, from the settlement it was handed: the adventure's id, the location, the job, the outcome, each stage's result, what was credited, and the party in the order it set out, each hero by its name, unit type, sprite set, the level it came home or died at, and whether it died, with no hero id and no owner. `recentAdventuresAt(location)` is one strongly consistent Query, newest first, of at most 20 items, so a player who has just settled finds the adventure there; it drops the expired, and an adventure whose job this deploy's content does not know, which nothing could name and whose replay is another version's (the engine's `jobOf`, the one lookup of a job by its id). On the graph it answers `[RecentAdventure!]` to any signed-in player: `id`, `job`, `outcome`, `stages`, `credited`, `party` and `settledAt`. Proven by data-layer tests of the item as stored with no id of a player or a hero in it, one item for a repeated and a racing submit, a defeat, none for a discard, the order, the bound of one Query and 20 items, the expiry, and two adventures settled in one millisecond; the stack test settles the timber job and finds it in Millbrook's list by its id. The client is Issue-290.
- **Built (Issue-289, decision 0053).** The index and its read. `GSI1` on the location table, `GSI1PK` and `GSI1SK` as strings, projecting every attribute (`terraform/modules/environment/tables.tf`, and the tests' table in `packages/hub/test/setup/table.ts`); the hub's role may `Query` it, in a statement of its own on the index's ARN. `Repository.settle` sets two attributes on the record as it marks it settled, `settledAt` and `replayOf`, the job once more, and ElectroDB writes the index's keys from them, `JOB#<replayOf>` and `<settledAt>`; start and discard set neither, so an open or discarded record carries no key. `replayOf` is hidden, so no read answers it; why the index is keyed from it and not from `jobId` is decision 0053. `replaysOf(job)`, in `packages/hub/src/replays.ts`, is one Query of the index, newest first, of at most 20 records, every outcome. On the graph it answers `[Adventure!]` to any signed-in player, the record through the type `adventure(id)` serves a settled one by, hero ids included and no owner, and `Adventure` has `settledAt`, null until it settles; a job this deploy's content does not know is `BAD_USER_INPUT`. A list does not ask for `timeline`, which is one read of the bucket per adventure. Proven by data-layer tests of the keys as stored on an open, a settled and a discarded record, the settled record answered whole through the index, the order, every outcome, the bound of one Query and 20 records, and one job's replays apart from another's; the handler's test of the field, its refusals and no owner in the answer; and the stack test, which settles the timber job and reads its replays until its adventure is among them, with the function's own role.
