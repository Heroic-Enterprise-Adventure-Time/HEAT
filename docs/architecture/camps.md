# Camps, heroes and resources

The keys of the two tables and the transactions that write them: the camp, its facilities and holdings, heroes, the ledger, the heroes seen at a location, the location's recent adventures and replays, a player's unlocks, and, decided and not built, a caravan's cargo and passengers. The game's side is `docs/design/game.md`, "Locations and camps", "The heroes hall", "Resources", "Unlocks" and "Caravans"; the key shapes and their reasons are decisions 0026, 0029, 0050, 0053 and 0059. Why there are two tables is in `docs/architecture/overview.md`, "Services and data"; this was a section of that document until Issue-282.

Each item carries a status: **Decided**, **Working assumption** or **Open**, as `docs/design/game.md` defines them. An item marked **Built** names the issue that built it, and one marked **not built** the issues that will (`docs/README.md`, rule 15).

## The keys and what writes them

- **Decided.** The player table's keys, all in the player's one item collection, `PLAYER#<id>`, so each read is one Query of it by a sort-key prefix: a camp's items by `AT#`, and the player's camps by that and one more, of `UNLOCK#` (the item built by Issue-351, below):

  | Sort key | Item |
  | --- | --- |
  | `PROFILE` | the player |
  | `AT#<location>#CAMP` | the camp: hall and warehouse levels, the counts of active and of living heroes, kept by every transaction that changes them (recruit and retire so far); no item means level 1 with no heroes, at the starting location and at any location whose unlock the player holds (decision 0059) |
  | `UNLOCK#<unlock>` | an unlock the player holds: when it was first earned and the adventure that earned it; written at settlement only where absent, and never deleted (decision 0059) |
  | `AT#<location>#FACILITY#<kind>` | an own facility the camp builds and its level, absent until built; the hall's and the warehouse's levels are on the camp's item (decision 0032) |
  | `AT#<location>#HOLDING#<resource>` | a resource balance at that location |
  | `HERO#<heroId>` | a hero, with its location as an attribute, since a caravan moves it; where it was recruited, `recruitedIn`, which nothing changes; and the open adventure's id while it is on it |
  | `LEDGER#<causeId>#<entry>` | append-only; the cause is an adventure id, a hero id or a build, `build-<location>-<kind>-<level>`, and the entry can exist once; an adventure's credits are `credit#<material>` entries under its id, and a caravan's `load`, `deliver` and `return` entries too (Issue-354) |
  | `OPEN` | the one open adventure |

- **Decided.** A hero's item: unit type, sprite set and name, chosen by the hub at recruitment and never by the client; location; where and when it was recruited; level, XP and the stat-boost picks (the choices, not the resulting numbers, so they survive a change to the stat tables); weapon and its fights left; status, alive, dead or retired, and when it retired; the open adventure it is on, set at start and cleared at settlement, while it is on one; history counters. Fields a later feature adds default in code.
- **Decided.** Recruiting is one mutation, `recruitHero(location, unitType, requestId)`, and one transaction: the camp's active count below the hall's cap, then incremented; one conditional debit and one ledger entry per resource in the cost, or none for a villager when the camp's count of living heroes is zero, which the condition checks; the hero item put if absent. The request id becomes the hero id, so a repeated request fails its condition and returns the hero already made. The hub draws the sprite set and then a name that suits its label (masculine, feminine or either; game.md, "Heroes") from the static content with a cryptographic random source, avoiding names on the camp's living roster. Its input names the camp's location (decision 0059, Issue-351); before that it recruited at the starting location alone.
- **Decided.** The profile is made on first sign-in by `joinGame`, which the client calls after every sign-in: one update that writes the profile if absent and leaves it alone if present. Nothing else creates a profile. A Cognito trigger is not used: every sandbox stack shares one user pool (decision 0022), so a trigger cannot know which stack's table to write, and the trigger after sign-up fires once per user, not once per stack.
- **Built (Issue-81).** The keys above for the camp, the hero, the holdings and the ledger; `joinGame`; `recruitHero` for the free villager, with the cap and the free-villager rule as conditions on the camp's counts, proven by data-layer tests under concurrent recruits and repeated requests; the player's camps and heroes on `me`, owner-only; the static content of Millbrook, the first location, in `packages/engine/src/content.ts`. The stack test recruits the free villager and checks that a second is refused. The client (Issue-81, second pull request): the header bar with the location select and the open adventure; `joinGame` after every sign-in; the camp scene in PixiJS (decision 0028) with the ground, the hall, the job board and the warehouse, and the living heroes walking about; the hall's roster and the free villager, who walks out of the hall; a hero's card on a click. The page's regions (Issue-89, `docs/design/game.md`, "The page"): the facility menu with its icons, the persistent content area with the panel as a child route of the camp (`/camp/<location>/<facility>`, `/camp/<location>/heroes-hall/heroes/<id>`, `packages/client/src/app/app.routes.ts`), each panel its own component under `camp/panels/`, the narrow-screen order, and the scene filling its box around Millbrook's 400×225 map, in `packages/client/src/app/camp/`, with the arithmetic in `page-layout.ts`. Millbrook lists a mine, a lumber camp and a caravan hub as `buildableFacilities` in the engine's content, each with its description in `FACILITIES`; they stand at level 0 until built (Issue-93).
- **Built (Issue-98).** Retiring is one mutation, `retireHero(heroId)`, and one transaction, `packages/hub/src/retire.ts`: the hero's status set to retired, with the time, on the condition that it is still the status read, alive or dead; the camp's active count decremented, and its living count too if the hero was alive, on the condition that neither goes below zero. A concurrent retire, death or move fails the hero's condition, so the counts change once; a recruit that read the camp before a retire fails its own condition on the counts. A hero already retired is answered as it is. Retiring the only living hero makes the next villager free. A hero on the open adventure cannot retire (Issue-113). Proven by data-layer tests under concurrent retires and a retire racing a recruit for the last slot; the stack test retires its villager and recruits the free villager again on a sandbox stack. The client: a Retire button on an active hero's card, behind a confirmation in place; a retired hero's card says when it retired and offers nothing; the hall's roster lists the retired apart, collapsed; and a retired hero walks back into the hall and leaves the scene.
- **Built (Issue-82).** Paid recruitment and the roster. `recruitHero` recruits any unit type on the location's list at its hall level, `Repository.recruit` in `packages/hub/src/db/repository.ts`: one transaction puts the hero, counts it into the camp on the condition that the hall is at the level read and below its cap, debits each resource of the cost on the condition that the balance covers it with a ledger entry `LEDGER#<heroId>#debit#<material>` that can exist once, and puts the sighting. The price is one rule in the engine, `recruitCost`, which the hub charges and the hall shows: a villager is free when the camp has no living hero and costs its price otherwise, and only the villager's transaction holds the living count, zero for a free one and above zero for a paid one, so a death or a recruit between the read and the write cannot change what was charged. Refusals, as data (decision 0038): `HallLevel`, `HallFull`, `InsufficientResources` with `short` (each material's amount and the camp's balance), and `Conflict` only when a lost race is explained by no rule; a request that loses a race reads the camp again and is refused with the rule that now fails; a unit type the location does not recruit is `BAD_USER_INPUT`. Proven by data-layer tests of two recruits for the last slot, two for the timber of one, a paid recruit racing a retire for the last slot, a repeated paid request, and a paid fighter with ore put into the holdings directly, since no job yields ore until Issue-133. The stack test asks for a fighter and is refused for want of ore, and after the timber run recruits a paid villager for 20 timber and retires it, on every stack. The client: the hall's roster grouped living, dead and retired, the retired collapsed; every recruit the location offers, its price beside the camp's balance, and why it cannot be had; the hub's refusal shown in place on the recruit it refused.
- **Built (Issue-99).** Heroes in the camp scene are drawn at half the scene's scale, rounded up (`heroScale` in `packages/client/src/app/camp/page-layout.ts`; decision 0028, as amended by Issue-99): the canvas renders at the scene's scale, each hero on a whole canvas pixel, walking at its own art pixels' pace.
- **Built (Issue-351, decision 0059).** Unlocks, and Highcrag as the first location that needs one. One item per unlock the player holds, `UNLOCK#<unlock>` in the table above: the unlock, when it was first earned and the adventure that earned it.

  **The write.** The engine's result lists the unlocks a run earned, its job's on arrival for now, and `Repository.settle` writes one item for each the result lists, in its one transaction: an update with no condition whose every attribute is set only where it is absent, and no read of what the player holds. The first grant makes the item; a later one changes nothing on it and cancels nothing, so the unlock keeps its first time and its first adventure; a repeat of the same settlement is stopped by the record's condition as before. Nothing deletes one. `packages/hub/test/electrodb.test.ts` holds how ElectroDB writes it, which decision 0059 left to this issue to check.

  **The reads.** `Repository.unlocks` is one Query of the `UNLOCK#` items. `Repository.camps`, which `me.camps` answers, is the starting location's camp and one for each location whose unlock the player holds, in the content's order, from that Query and the one of the `AT#` items; a camp with no item is level 1 with no heroes, and items at a location the player has not unlocked are no camp. `Repository.camp` reads one location's items and no unlock.

  **The refusals.** A verb that takes a location, recruit, build and start, refuses a location the player has not unlocked twice (`docs/standards/graphql.md`, rule 9): by its read (`campLocation` in `packages/hub/src/locations.ts`), and by a condition check on the unlock's item in its transaction, written once for the three (`unlockHeld` in `packages/hub/src/db/repository.ts`); a location that needs no unlock costs neither. The refusal is `BAD_USER_INPUT`, as building's "no such camp" already was, and not a refusal type: the player's own camps never list a locked location and an unlock is never taken away, so a request that names one is the client's defect. `recruitHero` takes its location, and a request id reused for another location is `BAD_USER_INPUT`, compared with where the hero is, which is where it was recruited until a caravan moves one (Issue-354). A tab loaded before the deploy sends a recruit with no location and is refused before any resolver runs, with the `outdated` flag; it reloads at once, unless it has an adventure open or a result showing, and then it cannot recruit until that adventure is over, which the owner accepted (2026-11-24, the review of PR-359). The free villager stays a rule of a location that recruits villagers, which Highcrag does not: every recruit there costs its cost with no hero alive.

  **Who reads what.** `me.unlocks` serves the unlocks to their owner alone, each with its time and the adventure that first earned it, the unlock as the enum `Unlock`, built from the engine's list. A settled result is served to any signed-in player, so `AdventureResult.unlocks` lists what the run earned whether or not the player held it, and no public field says which were new. The page works that out from the owner's own unlocks (the owner, 2026-11-24): an unlock in a result was new exactly when the player's names that adventure, which holds across a repeat, a reload and a lost response, and stores nothing private beside the public result. A result settled before this deploy has no list and is read as having earned none, and an unlock a later deploy's content adds is left out by a deploy that does not know it. The feeds of a location, `heroesSeenAt` and `recentAdventuresAt`, answer any signed-in player for Highcrag as for any location, unlocked or not: the location table is public (decisions 0029 and 0043).

  Proven by data-layer tests, `packages/hub/test/unlocks.test.ts`: an unlock written once, by a repeated submit, by two that race, by a repeat that loses the race wherever the other commits and by a settlement cancelled and run again; a second adventure that earns it again, whose result lists it as the first one's does and whose settlement pays; none for a retreat, for a party that is all down or for a job that grants none; the player's camps before and after; each of the three verbs refused at Highcrag by its read, with no transaction made, and by its transaction when the read is made to say otherwise, and let through once the unlock is held; a recruit, a build and the board's job at Highcrag, each paid from and credited to Highcrag's own stock; no free hero there; and, for each of the three verbs at the starting location, no request to DynamoDB that names an unlock's item, a read or a transaction. The three are every mutation whose input names a location, which the test reads off the built schema. The handler's test serves the unlocks to their owner alone and a result's to another player. These tests hold the rule apart from the one job that grants an unlock: they settle a job they add to the content they pass `submitAdventure` (`docs/standards/graphql.md`, rule 27). The stack test checks that its user holds no unlock, has the starting camp alone and is refused a recruit at Highcrag: its user never builds the caravan hub, so it never scouts the road.
- **Built (Issue-352, decision 0059).** The job that grants the first unlock, and the page's second camp.

  **The job** is content alone: "Scout the road to Highcrag" in `packages/engine/src/content.ts`, posted by Millbrook's caravan hub, with `grants` naming the road's unlock. The hub starts it and settles it with what Issue-351 built, and needed no change: the start refuses it at a camp with no hub as it refuses any facility's job, and settlement writes the unlock its result lists.

  **The page.** The header's select lists the camps `me.camps` answers, so Highcrag is there once the unlock is held, and `Camp` sends the address of a location the player has no camp at to the starting camp once the player is read. A camp's scene is its location's layout, `LAYOUTS` in `packages/client/src/app/camp/scene/layout.ts`, one for every location of the content, each standing exactly the facilities the content gives a camp there, which `scene.spec.ts` holds; Highcrag's is the ground `crag-01` and the layout the owner chose in Issue-350's mock-up, with the same facility pictures as Millbrook's, a facility's picture being its kind's. A battle's road is the ground of the location its adventure left from (`groundAt`; decision 0028, as amended by Issue-352). The ground sets are one list, `GROUNDS` in `packages/client/src/app/ground-sets.ts`, which the art build checks what it copied against. The hall says why a camp with nobody alive is empty where its location offers no hero for nothing, which is the engine's own rule read over the location's list (`Game.noFreeRecruit`). Recruit and build send the camp on screen and a start the camp its panel is opened in, as before.

  **Which unlocks were new.** `Me` asks for `unlocks { adventureId unlock }`, read back into the engine's own at the boundary, a value this client's engine lacks passing through. The result page shows the content's words for each of the player's own unlocks that names the adventure just settled (`earnedWords` in `packages/client/src/app/adventure/result-words.ts`), once the player has been read again after the submit, and leaves out an unlock its content does not know. Settlement writes that name only for an unlock the adventure's result lists and the player did not hold, so those are exactly the unlocks of the result that were new. The page does not ask the result for its own list: the submit is the one operation that also goes to the previous deploy, which refuses a document that asks for a field newer than it (`docs/standards/angular.md`, rule 19).

  Proven by the engine's test of the job over 500 seeds for each of thirteen parties (`packages/engine/test/adventure/road-job.test.ts`); the hub's data-layer tests, `packages/hub/test/unlocks.test.ts`, "the road to Highcrag": the job refused at a camp with no hub, started and settled through the verbs at a camp that has built it, with the engine's own content, Highcrag then among the player's camps, a recruit there refused for want of resources, and a second run that changes nothing; and the client's specs of the select, the result's words, the empty hall, each panel's location, the layouts against the content, and the address of a camp the player does not have.
- **Built (Issue-354, decision 0059).** A caravan, a job that delivers to another location, in the transactions an adventure already has, with no new table, index or partition key. It replaces the working assumption this item was, a slot taken at the destination at the start and one of the two released by the outcome, with the whole of it.

  **The start,** beside its own writes: the destination's unlock checked; each resource of the cargo debited from the origin's holding on the condition that the balance covers it, with a ledger entry `LEDGER#<adventureId>#load#<material>`; the destination camp's count of active heroes raised by the heroes marked to stay, on the condition that the hall's cap there covers them; and the record's `caravan` attribute, the destination, the cargo and who stays. The party is validated from the origin camp's living heroes, as any party is. The raised count is the slot held at the destination, and the hero's slot at home is kept until the end, so a recruit at either camp while the caravan is out cannot take it (decision 0026). The cargo is at most what the cart carries, by the caravan hub's level.

  **The settlement,** by how the run ended, which the engine's result says with whether the cart was standing on arrival:

  | The run ends | Cargo | Heroes marked to stay | The destination's count of active heroes |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Arrives, cart standing | each resource credited to the destination's holding up to its warehouse's cap, on the condition that the balance and the warehouse's level are those read, with `LEDGER#<adventureId>#deliver#<material>` saying the credit and what the cap lost | `location` set to the destination, a dead one too; the origin's counts of active and of living heroes lowered, and the destination's count of living heroes raised by those who live | kept |
  | Arrives, cart broken | none | as above | kept |
  | Retreat or a time limit | none | stay home | lowered again |
  | All down | none | dead, at home, as any party's | lowered again |

  The drops, the enemies' XP and the bonus are the origin camp's, as every adventure's are, and the feed item is put under the origin alone. A hero who lands has its sighting deleted under the origin, and put under the destination only if it lives: a dead hero has no sighting, there as anywhere (decision 0029); the others are refreshed or deleted as today. A resource that is both dropped and delivered is two holdings, the origin's and the destination's, and two ledger entries, `credit` and `deliver`, so no two writes name one item, which a transaction refuses. One open adventure per player means one caravan in flight, so the held slots are always the open adventure's: the page reads them from it, and the camp's item has no count of its own for them. Once settled, the result says what was delivered and what was lost, and is public as every result is, with no owner; the cargo of a caravan still in flight is on an open record, which `adventure(id)` answers to its owner alone.

  **The size.** The open item, the record, the two camps, four heroes, two sightings for each hero who lands alive and one for each other, and the feed item are at most 17 items; each resource credited or delivered adds two, and each unlock the run earned adds one, held already or not, against the 100 a transaction takes (decision 0059, "Facts checked").

  **A discard** returns each resource of the cargo to the origin's holding up to its cap, with `LEDGER#<adventureId>#return#<material>`, and lowers the destination's count again, beside what a discard writes today. A balance that moves between its read and its write is read again, as settlement's is.

  **Where a hero was recruited.** A hero that moves keeps `recruitedIn`, the location it was recruited at, set by the recruit and never changed, so that a repeated recruit is compared with it and not with where the hero now is (`madeBy` in `packages/hub/src/recruit.ts`). A hero recruited before Issue-354 has no such attribute, and is read as recruited where it is, which is true until it first moves; the settlement that first moves it writes the attribute from its location then, only where it is absent. Nothing is migrated (Issue-370 holds migrations).

  **Built** in `packages/hub/src/start.ts`, `submit.ts`, `discard.ts` and the repository's `startAdventure`, `settle` and `discard`. The start's refusals: `InsufficientResources` with `short` for a cargo the origin cannot pay, `HallFull` for a destination hall with no room for everyone who stays, each also by its transaction's condition, and each answered with `AdventureOpen` where another start took the balance or the slot; a destination the player has not unlocked, a cargo no cart carries, a hero who stays and is not of the party, or a cargo or a hero who stays on a job that delivers nothing, is `BAD_USER_INPUT`. The cart's capacity is held in the transaction as the hub's level that carries the cargo, among the facilities the start checks. Proven by the data-layer tests of `packages/hub/test/caravan.test.ts`: every row of the table but the time limit, which no party of the content reaches (`packages/engine/test/adventure/caravan-job.test.ts`); each refusal by its read and by its transaction; a start racing another start, a spend and a recruit; a submit repeated and raced; the destination's cap part full and full; a resource dropped and delivered; a discard's return capped; a hero who lands dead; the cargo of an open caravan; a recruit repeated after its hero moved; and the largest settlement, a party of four landing, every resource credited and delivered and an unlock earned, measured at 34 items.
- **Built (Issue-93, decision 0032).** Building and upgrading a facility is one mutation, `buildFacility`, its input naming the camp, the facility and the level being built, so a repeat finds it built and is answered with the camp, charging nothing (`packages/hub/src/build.ts`). One transaction, `Repository.build`: the facility's level set to the next on the condition that it is still the level read, each facility the level needs checked at its level or above, and each resource of the cost debited on the condition that the balance covers it, with a ledger entry `LEDGER#build-<location>-<kind>-<level>#debit#<material>` that can exist once. The hall's and the warehouse's levels are on the camp's item, which the recruit and settle transactions already condition on; every other own facility is its own item, `AT#<location>#FACILITY#<kind>`, absent until built. The costs, levels and prerequisites are `FACILITIES` in the engine's content, and the reason a level cannot be built is one engine rule, `buildRefusal`, which the hub checks and the panel shows. Refusals, as data: `MaxLevel`, `Requires` with `missing`, `InsufficientResources` with `short`, `Conflict`; no such camp or facility, or a level that is not the next, is `BAD_USER_INPUT`. The camp answers each facility's level on `Camp.facilities`. Proven by data-layer tests of five concurrent builds of one level, two builds for the timber of one, a stale level, a stale balance, a hall upgrade racing a recruit and a requirement held in the transaction. The stack test builds the mine once its test user's camp can pay, and checks that a repeat charges nothing and that the mine has no second level (it gains one by decision 0059, not built, Issue-357, which changes that check). The client: a build section in each own facility's panel and in the hall's, and the scene swapping a facility's picture when its level changes.
- **Built (Issue-133).** A job names the facility that posts it, `facility` in the engine's `Job`, or none for the job board; `jobsPostedBy(location, poster)` is the one rule for which jobs a poster lists, which the panels show. The first is the mine's "Ore from the hill", which pays for a fighter (decision 0031); the lumber camp's is "Lumber from the deep woods" (Issue-291), added as content alone, and since then the data-layer tests below run for every facility of Millbrook that posts a job, read from the content, with one more: a facility's job is refused at a camp that has built every other such facility and not its own. `startAdventure` finds the job anywhere at the location and refuses a facility's job at a camp that has not built the facility with `Requires`, the facility in `missing`, as building does; the start's transaction checks the facility's item, `AT#<location>#FACILITY#<kind>`, at level 1 or above, so a start that read the facility built and finds no item is refused when it reads again. `Job.facility` says the poster on the graph. Proven by data-layer tests of the refusal, of the start once the mine is built, and of the condition held in the transaction against a camp read with the mine; the engine's test replays the job over 500 seeds with a lone villager and with a fighter and a villager. The client: the mine's panel lists its jobs once built, a job's detail sits under its poster, `/camp/<location>/mine/jobs/<id>`, and the job board lists only its own. The stack test is refused the job while its camp has no mine, and once it has one plays it, recruits a fighter for the ore and retires it.
- **Working assumption.** A credit past the warehouse cap stops at the cap. DynamoDB cannot take a minimum inside an update, so settlement reads the balances, computes the capped credits and writes them on the condition that each balance is unchanged, retrying on a conflict; the ledger records the amount lost.
- **Decided (decision 0030).** What settlement writes besides the credits, all in the one transaction: to each surviving hero the XP of every enemy that went down and, on arrival, the job's bonus, a retreat keeping the enemies' XP alone (decision 0058, Issue-339), with its level from a threshold table in the static content, and no stat-boost picks yet (game.md, "Heroes", keeps the picks; they come with a later issue); a hero at zero health when the adventure ends is dead, so its status becomes dead, the camp's living count is decremented on the condition that it is above zero, and its sighting is deleted; each survivor's sighting is refreshed (decision 0029). The result the response carries says per stage how it ended, and overall the rewards, the amount lost at the cap, the XP and the dead. Decision 0059 adds to it the unlocks the run earned, written by any settlement (built, Issue-351), and a caravan's cargo and its heroes who stay (built, Issue-354); both are the items above.
- **Decided.** The heroes of other players shown in a camp are a bounded sample, read from the location table (decision 0029):

  | PK | SK | Item |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `LOCATION#<location>` | `SEEN#<heroId>` | a hero seen there: name, unit type, sprite set and level, never its owner; `expiresAt`, the table's TTL attribute, a week after it was seen |

  A hero is seen when it does something: recruiting puts its sighting and retiring deletes it, each in the hero's own transaction; settlement refreshes a survivor's and deletes a dead hero's (Issue-114). Opening or switching camps is not a sighting. `heroesSeenAt(location)` reads at most 20 items from a random point among the sightings, wrapping round once, drops the expired, which DynamoDB deletes only within days, and the caller's own heroes, and returns up to 6 at random.
- **Built (Issue-100).** The sighting in the recruit and retire transactions, the TTL on the location table, and `heroesSeenAt` in `packages/hub/src/seen.ts`, proven by data-layer tests of the bound, the expiry and the exclusion of the caller's heroes. The client reads the sample when the player signs in or changes camps, not after each recruit, walks it about the scene beside the player's own heroes, and opens one on a read-only card, `/camp/<location>/seen/<id>`, with its name, unit type and level.
- **Decided (decision 0050).** The adventures recently completed at a location, built by Issue-288, and the replays of a job, built by Issue-289, both in the location table:

  | Key | Item |
  | --- | --- |
  | `LOCATION#<location>` / `DONE#<settledAt>#<adventureId>` | an adventure completed there: the job, the outcome, each stage's ending, what was credited and the party's public fields, never its owner; `expiresAt` a week after it settled |
  | `GSI1`: `GSI1PK = JOB#<jobId>`, `GSI1SK = <settledAt>` | the adventure record, from settlement on; an open or discarded record carries neither attribute and is not in the index |

  The settle transaction puts the feed item and sets the record's index keys; a discard writes neither. `recentAdventuresAt(location)` reads the newest 20 of the location's `DONE#` items and drops the expired (Issue-288); `replaysOf(job)` reads the newest 20 of the job's records from the index, which projects every attribute and is eventually consistent (Issue-289). Both need a signed-in player and serve public fields only: the feed item stores nothing else, and the record keeps its player's id, which no field serves (decision 0043). The client lists them on the job board's panel and on a job's page, and plays any settled adventure's timeline on a page of its own (Issue-290). Nothing settled before the deploy is backfilled into either. A player's own history is not served by the index; it is designed with its feature, in the player table. The prefixes `FACILITY#` and `CONTRIB#` in the location's collection are kept for step 5's shared facilities, which are not designed.
