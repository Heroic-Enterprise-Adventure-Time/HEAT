# The game

What the player does in HEAT. How the system delivers it is in `docs/architecture/overview.md`. Nothing described here is built yet unless it says so.

Each item carries a status. **Decided** items are settled by the project owner. **Working assumption** items were proposed during design and not yet objected to: build on them, but expect them to be revisited. **Open** items are not yet answered. An item marked "not built" is decided and names the issue that builds it; until that issue is merged, the items it replaces say what the game does (`docs/README.md`, rule 15).

## The business

- **Decided.** The player runs an adventuring business.
- **Decided.** Heroes are close to unit types: every hero of a unit type starts with the same stats and abilities as every other, and what changes afterwards changes numbers, never abilities (see "Heroes"). Adding a unit type should be simple.
- **Decided (direction, if time allows).** Progression means gaining unit types that get more done with less intervention, automating what the player used to do by hand and freeing them for the next problem.
- **Decided.** A **job** is the template; an **adventure** is one run of a job. The names may change.
- **Decided.** Jobs are more than battles. Examples: take a cart of goods from one settlement to another; go into the forest, chop lumber and return safely; fight through a hundred goblins and kill their king. An adventure progresses linearly, doing things along the way.
- **Working assumption.** An outcome is richer than win or lose: how far the party got, what it gathered or delivered, who was lost.
- **Decided.** The villager is the starting hire: cheap to hire, with no specialized skills. Specialized laborers are unit types of their own: the miner and the lumberjack, recruited at Millbrook (Issue-287, "The heroes hall").

## Heroes

What makes one hero different from another of the same unit type. Nothing here changes a unit type's AI or gives a hero an ability its unit type lacks: every unit type keeps one way of choosing its actions.

- **Decided.** Appearance is cosmetic. At recruitment a hero is given a sprite set, drawn at random from the sets its unit type allows, and a name, drawn at random from a fixed list. A set may serve more than one unit type. The hero keeps both for good.
- **Decided.** The name suits the sprite. Every sprite set and every name is labelled masculine, feminine or either. A masculine set takes a masculine or either name, a feminine set a feminine or either name, and an either set any name. The name is drawn after the set, and avoids the names of the camp's living heroes while any are left. Heroes recruited before the labels keep their names. There is no renaming, so no text a player wrote is ever shown to another player, and the game needs no moderation.
- **Decided.** Advancement. A hero earns XP in adventures, credited at settlement, and XP brings levels. Each level offers a choice between small, fixed stat boosts from the unit type's list, so two heroes of the same unit type and level can differ.
- **Decided (direction).** Advanced classes. A hero may later move to an advanced class, which swaps its unit type and starts it again at level one. A temporary dip in strength is acceptable; an advanced class at its top level beats its base class at its top level.
- **Decided.** Weapons. Each unit type uses one weapon kind and no other: rangers use bows, knights use swords. A weapon changes numbers, never abilities. Weapons are crafted from materials; a rare weapon needs at least one rare material.
- **Working assumption.** A weapon lasts a number of fights, a fight being one battle stage. A hero whose weapon breaks fights on with its unit type's unarmed numbers, so a broken weapon never strands a party.
- **Decided.** Death. A hero can die for good. Bringing a dead hero back always costs a resource or a consumable and needs a facility. Cheaper forms carry a penalty: lost XP, a lost level, or time to recover. The cheapest form comes early in the camp's progression, so losing a villager on a boss run hurts without being frustrating. A dead hero stays on the record with its history.
- **Working assumption.** Consumables. Crafted from materials and taken on an adventure in a small pack chosen at the start, which goes into the adventure's snapshot. Each is used through an override: party-wide heals, shields and bonuses that let a player spend their way through a hard mission. Unused consumables return to the player's holdings at settlement.
- **Decided.** History. Each hero keeps a record of what it has done, such as adventures survived, monsters defeated and goods delivered.
- **Open.** The stat boosts each unit type offers, the XP curve, which advanced classes exist, and the costs of resurrection, weapons and consumables.

## How the game looks

The rules for making a hero's sprite set are `docs/process/assets.md`, rules 8 to 13, for a camp's ground and facilities rules 14 to 19, for the page's icons rules 25 to 27, for the battle's monsters and props rules 28 to 31, and for the weapons and effects of an action rules 32 to 35; this section says what the player sees.

- **Decided.** One view for camp and battle: low top-down, about 20 degrees, the classic 3/4 view of an RPG town. Battle arranges the same sprites so that it reads as side-on. Two pictures are not in that view, both since the art of Issue-350 (`docs/process/assets.md`, rules 14 and 31): a prop that stands on the party's line, the caravan's cart, is drawn from the side, because it has one picture where a hero has a frame that faces east; and a ground made since the service changed is drawn from higher up, which flat ground does not show.
- **Decided.** An NES-era look, with Final Fantasy 1 as the reference: warm, earthy colours, a black outline, few colours per sprite, characters 32 px tall.
- **Decided.** Characters, monsters, environment and UI share one theme. Ranger 01 (`art/heroes/ranger-01/`) is the baseline.
- **Decided.** Characters hold nothing. Every action, whatever the ability, is the hero's act animation, a three-frame lead jab whose middle frame is held for as long as the action lasts, played with two separate sprites: the weapon, which appears beside the hero, and the ability's effect, such as a slash, an arrow or a spell. A weapon's one sprite is also its icon in the inventory. Its motion is drawn as animation frames, not rotated in code, because rotated pixel art blurs at any angle but a right angle.
- **Decided.** A sprite set's animations are walk, stance, act, hit and down. A hero standing outside battle is its still frame; there is no idle animation.
- **Decided.** A camp stands on a 16 px tiled ground with no outline, so that the outlined buildings and heroes stand off it. Each facility is one sprite at a three-quarter angle, front wall facing down-right, the same for every facility. A facility's level may change its picture, never its size or its angle (decision 0027).
- **Decided.** The page's icons, such as a facility's entry in the menu or a resource, and the favicon, are 32 px pixel art in the same look, drawn at a whole-number scale. Hide, timber and ore have theirs, picked from one pack so that they share a style (Issue-340, Issue-344), and the battle scene's picture of a drop is the first thing to draw a resource's icon; the warehouse and a cost do not yet. What the scene says in characters is pixel art too: the digits of a drop's amount are sprites cut from a pixel font, at the scene's scale, not text the browser sets (Issue-344). The favicon is a gold coin with a sword through it: money and a weapon, the business of adventuring (Issue-89).

## The page

How the page around the scene is laid out (Issue-89). The scene's own rules are in "Locations and camps" and decision 0028.

- **Decided.** Four regions, and nothing overlaps. The **header bar**: the title, the camp select, the open adventure and sign-in, nothing more; resources are not shown there, because a camp will hold many kinds. The **facility menu**: an entry with an icon for every facility the camp has or may build, the same list the scene draws. The **stage**: the scene, filling the space the other regions leave it. The **content area**: always one panel, such as the heroes hall, the job board, the warehouse or a hero's card; the heroes hall when nothing else is chosen.
- **Decided.** Where there is room, the menu is on the left, the stage in the middle and the content area on the right, beside the scene and never over it. The side columns give way as the window narrows, not only the scene: the content area takes about a quarter of the window, between 17rem and 28rem, and the menu is an icon rail that shows its labels only where the wider menu costs the scene no scale step, with its icons at twice the size beside a scene at scale 3 or more. Where side by side would leave the scene below scale 2, on a tablet, a small window or a phone, the regions stack: the scene at the top, the page's width, then the menu as a row of icons, then the content area, which scrolls with the page.
- **Decided.** Where the player is, is the address's path: the camp, then its panel, `/camp/<location>/<facility>`, with a hero's card under the hall, `/camp/<location>/heroes-hall/heroes/<id>`. The browser's back returns to the last panel, a reload keeps it, a panel can be linked to, and the menu marks the hall while one of its heroes is open. A panel moves from a list to a detail and back, as the hall's roster does to a hero's card. The open adventure is `/adventure`, and any settled adventure's replay `/replays/<id>`, each outside any camp, because the battle takes the stage (Issue-290).
- **Decided.** What was done at a location is listed where it was done (Issue-290, decision 0050). The job board's panel ends with "Recently here": up to ten of the location's adventures from the last week, newest first, each led by its job, then how it ended, how long ago, and its party, each hero with its unit type, its level and whether it died. A job's page ends with "Replays", under the party pick, the same lines led by how long ago. Each line opens the adventure's replay page, which plays it with the pause and speeds of the player's own replay, beside where and when, how it ended, its stages and its party, and says "replay unavailable" for one stored before the battles changed. A list is read when its panel opens, not after each of the player's actions, and a list that cannot be read says so and leaves the rest of the panel working.
- **Decided.** A facility the camp has not built stands in the scene as an unfinished building, its level 0, and is in the menu like any other. Its panel says what it will do.
- **Decided.** Building is done from the facility's panel (Issue-93, decision 0032). The panel of a facility not built shows what building it costs, each resource beside the camp's balance, and a Build button; the panel of a built one, the heroes hall's and the warehouse's included, its level and its next level the same way, with what the level gives, such as "Holds 6 heroes". The button is disabled with the reason when the camp cannot build it: another facility needed first, or a resource short. A refusal from the hub is shown in place. At its highest level, the panel says so. A build shows at once in the scene, the facility's picture swapped in place.
- **Decided.** Resources are shown where they matter, not in a bar: the warehouse lists the camp's stock against its cap, and every cost or reward shows the camp's balance beside it.
- **Decided.** In battle the content area is reserved for the party and the controls instead of covering the scene, which scales into what is left; on a narrow screen they go below it.

## The adventure loop

- **Decided.** An adventure runs deterministically in the browser on a timestamped timeline and is submitted in one piece for verification. The game is not offline-first; the aim is to avoid a request per click.
- **Decided.** The timeline's clock counts whole milliseconds. Charge timers and turns run on that integer clock.
- **Decided.** The player can interject actions that may override a hero's next action. An override waits for the hero's next turn and applies if it is allowed at that moment; if it is not, the hero follows its own logic. Overrides go through the seeded run like everything else, so the timeline stays deterministic.
- **Decided.** No real-time gating. Players can pause, and can fast-forward, including through content they have automated.
- **Decided.** One unresolved adventure per player at a time.
  - It must be easy for the player to find, so they know it is in progress and can return to it.
  - If the browser closes, the adventure waits. On next sign-in the app checks for an unresolved adventure and picks it up, replaying the overrides so far with the client's own engine.
  - If the player never returns, it stays unresolved.
  - An adventure survives one game update and not two: the update before the current one stays able to verify it, so a player in the middle of an adventure when an update ships finishes and submits as if nothing happened. After a second update it is discarded, and the player is told (decision 0030, amending 0003).
  - A player who dislikes how a run is going must finish it to continue, or wait for the next update.
- **Decided.** A replay is what the server verified, played back. It is display only: any version of the game plays it, and it never needs the engine again.

## The battle

How an adventure plays out: the first cut, agreed 2026-10-21 (decision 0030), with resource nodes reworked on 2026-11-10 (decision 0051) and ranged units added on 2026-11-11 (decision 0052). What an adventure yields was decided again on 2026-11-20 (decision 0058): drops by gathering trait, enemy XP and the one retreat rule are built (Issue-339), so are hide and skinning, the first new pair (Issue-340), and so are jobs that vary (Issue-341), with the lumber camp's first job the first one written for that shape (Issue-291). The shape is decided; the numbers are working assumptions in the engine's static content and are expected to change as the game is played.

- **Decided.** A job is an ordered list of stages: travel, fight, gather and arrive. An adventure runs them on one timeline, with health, what has dropped and the enemies' XP carried between stages. A fight or a gather lists what stands on the other side, monsters and resource nodes together, and ends when all of it is down (2026-11-10, Issue-283, decision 0051). A tree is a resource node that drops timber when it is felled, and an ore vein one that drops ore when it is broken (decision 0058). A cart is a party member with health and no actions, for caravans: decided, not built (Issue-353; "Caravans" below). The stages are the engine's: the page does not list them ("Not every drop is told" below).
- **Decided.** Active time. Each unit has a charge timer that fills at its speed. When it fills, the unit picks the first usable action from its unit type's list, with the target the ability's rule chooses, warms up, resolves, and the timer resets. Nothing waits for the player: the battle plays itself, and the player watches, pauses, fast-forwards and interjects.
- **Decided.** One action list per unit type, fixed. The list is the unit's whole intelligence, and a better unit type is a better list, which is how progression automates what the player used to do by hand ("The business"). The player never edits a list.
- **Decided.** A marching order, not a grid. Each side has four lines, front, near, far and back; a line holds any number of units, with no order within it. A unit type has a home line, where it starts; the party pick places nobody. Reach is by line: an ability says which enemy lines it reaches from which of its own. When a side's front line empties, every unit on that side moves up a line, so the nearest occupied line is always the front. Stepping up is also an action, for the unit types whose list says so.
- **Working assumption.** The first unit types and monsters. Not very tactical, by design: a villager walks up and swings, because the player is not meant to keep villagers for long. Decision 0059 puts a caravan's cart first in the bandit's list, when the cart is in its reach: decided, not built (Issue-353; "Caravans" below).

  | Unit | Home line | Its list, first usable action wins |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Villager | near | strike the closest in reach; chop a tree if one is there; mine an ore vein if one is there; move up |
  | Miner | near | mine an ore vein if one is there; chop a tree if one is there; strike the closest in reach; move up |
  | Lumberjack | near | chop a tree if one is there; mine an ore vein if one is there; strike the closest in reach; move up |
  | Fighter | front | guard an ally below half health, if not already guarding; strike the closest |
  | Ranger | far | shoot the closest, on any line |
  | Wolf | front | bite the closest; move up |
  | Bandit | near | strike the weakest in reach; move up |
  | Goblin rock thrower | far | throw a rock at the closest, on any line |

- **Decided.** Ranged units (2026-11-11, Issue-286, decision 0052). A ranged attack reaches every line of the other side from every line of its own, where melee reaches only the front. So nodes hold the other side's front line and goblins stand behind them, throwing rocks at the heroes who chip at the nodes, out of reach until the nodes fall and their side moves up, unless the party brings an archer or two to deal with them (the owner's picture). A ranger shoots monsters before nodes, like any unit; with nothing else left it shoots a node, which costs the party nothing, because no hit gathers anything and a node drops by the traits of the heroes standing when it falls (decision 0058, replacing the waste of a shot that decision 0052 accepted). **Working assumption,** the numbers: a ranger has 26 health, speed 10 and damage 6, and its shot warms up for 700 ms; a goblin rock thrower has 12 health, speed 8 and damage 5, and its throw warms up for 600 ms.

- **Decided.** A guard lasts a few seconds. While it holds, hits aimed at the guarded ally land on the guard instead, and the guard does not start another until it expires (2026-10-21, Issue-110).
- **Decided.** Resource nodes, trees and ore veins, are obstacles (2026-11-10, Issue-283, decision 0051, replacing the rule of Issue-110). A node stands on the other side's lines like a monster and never acts. It has health, and any hit does its normal damage to it; no hit gathers anything, and what a node gives is its drops, when it goes down ("Drops by trait" below; decision 0058, which replaced decision 0051's difficulty, maximum yield, harvest by gather rating and waste). A regular attack is aimed at a monster first and at a node only when nothing else is in reach. Chop and mine stay as abilities for their look, each a strike at a node of its kind with a strike's damage and warmup, whoever swings. A node is worked only when no monster is in the ability's reach: with a wolf beside a tree a chop has no target, in a unit's list and forced alike, so nobody turns its back on what is biting it (2026-11-22, the owner's call on Issue-341; decision 0058, its second amendment). A gathering ability works the node with the least left, so a gatherer fells one tree before starting the next, rather than spreading its work over every tree in reach (2026-10-22, the owner's call on Issue-15).
- **Working assumption.** A unit's stats are health, speed (turns per ten seconds), damage and its gathering traits, each with a level ("The traits" below). The first numbers: a villager has 30 health, speed 8 and damage 4, and a miner and a lumberjack have the same (Issue-287); a fighter has 50 health, speed 10 and damage 6; a tree and an ore vein have 40 health each. A specialist works its own material first: its list has its own gathering ability before the other and before a strike. With a monster in reach it strikes that first, as a villager does, since a node is not worked then. On the goblins' mixed stage that costs nothing, because the goblins are out of a melee hero's reach until the veins fall: a specialist in a villager's place loses where villagers lose and wins where they win, with no more deaths (checked over 500 seeds, the review of PR-304, and again by Issue-339). The numbers are in the engine's static content.
- **Decided.** Two kinds of override: forcing a unit's next action, which applies at its next turn if it is usable then and is dropped silently if not; and party orders, press on and retreat. Press on is the default. Retreat can be selected at any time and stays selected. During a fight it is attempted; when a stage completes with it selected, the party retreats for certain (2026-10-21, Issue-110; built in Issue-112).
- **Decided.** Retreat. A retreat keeps what dropped and the XP of the enemies that went down, and loses the job's pay and its bonus: one rule for every job (decision 0058, built by Issue-339, replacing the penalty each job stated under decision 0030). No job states a penalty of its own, a caravan included: its cargo is not a drop, and a retreat abandons it (decision 0059; not built, Issue-354; "Caravans" below).
- **Working assumption.** A retreat is attempted every so often until it succeeds, each attempt rolled with the adventure's seeded random number generator. Bonuses and items raise the chance, and the chance rises with each failed attempt, so bad luck alone cannot trap a party. The first numbers: an attempt every 2 s from the order, 30% at first and 20 points more after each failure, so the fifth is certain.
- **Decided.** Every fight and gather stage carries its own time limit, on the adventure's own clock, because some are expected to resolve faster than others. Reaching it ends the adventure as a retreat, which keeps what the retreat rule above keeps. It is a deadlock kill switch, not a mechanic: the timer is shown only in its last ten seconds of simulated time, as a countdown in whole seconds, and nothing is designed to be beaten against it. The countdown is the owner's call on Issue-15, amending the first cut, which showed no number.
- **Decided.** Mana and status effects are left out until the loop works.
- **Decided.** The first job cannot be failed by a new player's free villager alone, whatever the player forces it to do (2026-10-22, Issue-126, decision 0031). "Pick up sticks": travel, fell one tree, arrive. It keeps a time limit like every gather stage, set far beyond what the job takes. It brings home timber and no pay, and the timber pays for the next villager. One time in ten a wolf is beside the tree ("A job's adventures vary" below; Issue-341): the villager strikes the wolf first and beats it with a little health left, and no forced action changes that, because a node is not worked while a monster is in reach. Checked with the wolf on every one of 5,000 seeds, the villager left alone and forced to each of its abilities every turn.
- **Working assumption.** Millbrook's board, easiest first (retuned 2026-11-10, decision 0051, and 2026-11-20, decision 0058). "Pick up sticks": one tree, which drops a villager 30 to 36 timber, and 5 XP; one time in ten a wolf beside it, for 5 XP more, 10 in all, which a lone hero of any type the camp recruits beats on every seed tried, and which leaves a ranger a hide about half the time. "Timber from the near woods": travel, fell two trees, fight a wolf on the way back, arrive; what the trees drop and a small pay. "Wolves on the mill road": travel, fight two wolves, arrive; paid in cloth. A party is one to four heroes. A job's pay is a reward from whoever posted it, so it need not be loot. The first numbers: the timber job drops villagers 60 to 72 timber and pays 3 cloth and 15 XP; the wolves job pays 10 cloth and 20 XP. A lone villager completes the first two on every seed tried and dies on the third; a fighter with a villager, or three villagers, clear it. A party with a ranger brings hide home from it too, which no job says (Issue-340): a fighter with a ranger completes it on every seed with no death, with no hide on about one run in four, one on half and two on a quarter; the timber job and the ore job each end in one wolf, and give a ranger one hide on about half their runs. The board lists each job's pay and its bonus XP, and never what it brings home ("Not every drop is told" below). The mine posts its own jobs (Issue-133): "Ore from the hill", travel, dig out two ore veins, fight a wolf on the way back, arrive; it drops villagers 40 to 48 ore and gives 15 XP, with no pay. One or two villagers complete it on every seed tried; a fighter with a villager completes it too and brings home the same ore, since one carrier of a trait is enough, and a fighter alone completes it with none. The mine's second job is the first with a mixed stage (Issue-286, decision 0052): "Goblins in the old workings", travel, dig out three ore veins with two goblin rock throwers behind them, arrive; 25 XP, with no pay. One to three villagers are defeated on every seed tried, and so is a fighter with one villager; four villagers complete it with 60 to 72 ore and lose one of them on most runs; a ranger with two villagers completes it on every seed with no death and the same 60 to 72 ore, and with a miner in one villager's place 84 to 96. A ranger with one villager completes it too, and on the few seeds where the villager falls before a vein does it brings home no ore: the carrier is worth protecting. The lumber camp posts its own job too (Issue-291), the first written for decision 0058's shape and not converted to it: "Lumber from the deep woods", travel, fell three trees or four with a wolf or two among them on half its runs, arrive; no pay, and 15 XP at its least roll and 28 at its largest. It drops villagers 90 to 144 timber and a lumberjack 126 to 200, so its worst roll beats the timber job's best. A lone hero of any type the camp recruits completes it with no wolf or one; two wolves, about one run in four, defeat a lone villager, lumberjack, miner or ranger on every seed tried and a lone fighter on about half of them. Two villagers lose one of their number on about one run in ten. Three villagers, a fighter with a villager, and a lumberjack with a fighter complete it on every seed with no death; a lumberjack with a fighter and a ranger does too, with the same timber and a hide from a little under half the wolves it meets, the party that covers every drop. A lumberjack with a ranger alone is the worst pair: on the runs where the lumberjack falls and the ranger finishes, the trees that came down after drop nothing. Its description tells of the trees and of cutters who did not come back, and names neither the wolves nor their hides. Each job was played over 500 seeds per party (Issue-339; the lumber camp's by Issue-291).
- **Decided.** What an adventure yields (2026-10-21, Issue-112; the drops and the XP by decision 0058, built by Issue-339). Arriving pays the job's pay and what dropped, and to each surviving hero the XP of every enemy that went down and the job's bonus. A retreat, ordered or forced by a time limit, keeps what dropped and the enemies' XP and loses the pay and the bonus. A party that is all down keeps nothing. A hero sets out at full health, health carries from stage to stage, and a hero that falls sits out the rest of the job and is dead at its end. A fight or a gather ends when everything on the other side is down, and nothing limits what a party carries (2026-11-10, Issue-283, decision 0051, replacing the carry limit of Issue-112).
- **Decided.** An action shows its two sprites (Issue-130), and all of it plays as the action resolves (Issue-335, decision 0057). The unit's weapon appears in its leading hand and swings with the act; at the blow the ability's effect plays on whoever it reached, a slash for a strike, chips of wood for a chop, and the target is hit; the rest of the swing follows. Nothing of an action is drawn while it warms up: the unit stands as it stood until its action happens. The weapon goes by unit type for now, a sword for the fighter, a hand axe for the villager, a dagger for the bandit, and the wolf bites with nothing in hand; a hero's crafted weapon replaces it when equipment exists. A projectile is the target's effect flying from the actor to the target (Issue-286, decision 0052), and its flight is part of its action: the ranger's bow is drawn with the arrow on its string and looses it, the goblin's rock leaves its hand at the top of the throw, and where each lands its sparks or its grit play and the target is hit. The ranger shoots with a bow, and the goblin throws with nothing in hand.
- **Decided.** The battle reads left to right (the owner's call on Issue-15). The party runs on from the left and walks in place while the road scrolls by; at a fight or a gather the other side rushes on from the right as the road scrolls with it, so the party seems to rush to meet it. The rush is flavor: the adventure's clock waits until everyone is on, and only then does the fight begin.
- **Decided.** The clock pauses while an action is animated (2026-11-20, Issue-335, decision 0057). An action is one instant of the adventure's clock, and when it resolves that clock pauses while the whole of it is shown in one piece: the wind-up, the blow or the release, a projectile's flight, the ability's effect, the target's hit and a fall. One action is seen from its first frame to its last before the next thing happens, so the animations do not run over each other, and nothing in an animation stops while the clock stands. The events of one frame pause the clock once, for the longest of what they begin and not for the sum, and an entry in the same frame takes the pause's place. The pause is the page's, like the rush in. It is measured in the time the page shows, the wall clock times the speed, so it shortens at 2× and 4× as the animations do, and it does not run down while the player has paused. The engine's clock knows nothing of it: a stage's time limit and its countdown, the override log, verification and a stored replay are what they were, and a replay pauses as a run does. **Working assumption:** the pause is the whole of the animation, one share for every kind of clip, a number beside the scene's other timings, and a shot's wind-up and flight together last as long as its warmup, as decision 0052 timed them, 450 ms and 250 ms for the bow and its arrow, 270 ms and 330 ms for the throw and its rock; both are set by looking.
- **Decided.** Drops by trait (2026-11-20, Issue-336, decision 0058; built by Issue-339). An enemy, a monster or a resource node, lists zero or more drops, each a resource and the gathering trait that collects it: a tree gives timber to a party that can cut wood, a wolf a hide to a party that can skin. A trait has a level. Who hit what does not matter: when an enemy goes down, the highest level of the drop's trait among the heroes standing in that stage is read, and the drop is rolled for that level, its chance and its amount. A hero that is down does not count, and with no carrier there is no drop. So a fighter is no longer wasted on a resource run and one carrier of a trait is enough: the puzzle is to cover every drop that may show up with a party that still survives the fight (the owner). The roll is the enemy's own, from the adventure's seed, and nothing the player does in the fight changes it.
- **Decided.** The traits are named for the craft: woodcutting, mining and skinning. A trait is the unit type's, a number like its other stats. The villager has woodcutting 1 and mining 1, the lumberjack woodcutting 2 and the miner mining 2 (built by Issue-339). The ranger has skinning 1 and the villager cannot skin, so hide is the first drop a party gets nothing of without its carrier (built by Issue-340): a party with no ranger standing when a wolf falls brings home no hide, and is not told why. A specialist carries its own trait alone to start with, so a miner brings home no timber and a lumberjack no ore; a unit type added later may carry several, some at higher levels, as one of the ways it is made interesting (the owner, 2026-11-20). **Working assumption,** the first numbers: a tree drops 30 to 36 timber at woodcutting 1 and 42 to 50 at level 2, an ore vein 20 to 24 ore at mining 1 and 28 to 32 at level 2, each always; a wolf drops one hide half the time at skinning 1, and one or two, always, at level 2, which no unit type has yet. At its worst roll each step of the first progression is still paid by one run of the job before it.
- **Decided.** Not every drop is told (the owner, 2026-11-20; built by Issue-339). A job's description says what its author chooses: "Pick up sticks" says that timber is collected and that a hero who can cut wood is needed, and other jobs may say much less: no job says that a wolf leaves a hide, or who can take it (Issue-340). The job board and a job's page say the description, the pay and the bonus XP, and neither a yield nor the job's stages: travel adds nothing, an arrival is not a stage of its own, and a fight and a gather run alike, so the description is where a job says as much or as little as its author decides (the owner, 2026-11-20, Issue-339, amending decision 0058's "the stages by kind"). While an adventure is played the page names no stage either. The result says what dropped. A job's page shows the camp's balance and the warehouse's room for the pay alone, since the room for a drop would name it (the owner, 2026-11-20). The rest is the player's to find. Nothing protects it beyond the page: the drop tables are in the game's code, which anyone may read.
- **Decided.** A drop shows twice, and only when the party got it (the owner, 2026-11-20, Issue-339; the scene's part built by Issue-344). While an adventure is played, the party panel's "Carrying" line counts up the moment an enemy falls and something drops. In the scene, the resource's icon rises from the fallen enemy as it falls, with the amount on its lower right in pixel digits, the scene's first text, and is gone; the adventure's clock waits for the rise as it waits for the fall, for the longer of the two (decision 0057). Neither says anything for a drop the party could not gather, so the scene never shows a drop that was missed or hints at one. A replay shows the same, from the stored events. Where a fight follows a gather at once, the last node's drop rises as the road carries it off behind the party. **Working assumption,** set by looking (the owner, 2026-11-23): the icon rises 14 px in 0.6 seconds, quickly at first, and fades over the last of it.
- **Decided.** Enemies give XP, and a retreat keeps what was won (decision 0058; built by Issue-339). Each enemy, a node included, has XP; a hero that survives earns the XP of every enemy that went down, in full, and on arrival the job's bonus. A retreat keeps the drops and the enemies' XP and loses the pay and the bonus. A party that is all down keeps nothing. **Working assumption,** the numbers: a tree and an ore vein give 3 XP, a wolf and a goblin rock thrower 5, a bandit 6, and the bonus of each of the five jobs that had XP before the enemies did is what brings its XP to what it gave then, counting the enemies that always appear; the lumber camp's job, written since, has a bonus of 6, which brings its least roll to the ore job's 15 (Issue-291), and Highcrag's board job has the ore job's stages, its bonus of 4 and its 15 (Issue-351); an enemy that appears by chance adds its own.
- **Decided.** A job's adventures vary (decision 0058; built by Issue-341). Each entry of a fight or a gather stage has a count or a range for it, and may have a chance of appearing at all, so the adventures of a job have the same stages and about the same enemies, from a limited pool, and are not the same: gathering sticks is usually a tree, and once in a while a wolf. The roll is made from the adventure's seed, which is issued after the party is picked, so the player picks without seeing it. A job may have no variance. Every fight and gather stage has something that always appears, its time limit fits its largest roll, and that roll brings home no more of a resource than a level-1 warehouse holds, unless the owner has accepted more for the job. The lumber camp's job is the one that has (the owner, 2026-11-23, Issue-291; decision 0058, its fourth amendment): the board's timber job already reaches a level-1 warehouse's 100 at a lumberjack's best roll, so a job with clearly more timber passes it, and this one's timber is held to a level-2 warehouse's 200, its every other resource to a level-1 warehouse like any job's. A camp that has not built the warehouse's second level loses what is over, and the result says how much; the job's page gives no warning, since the room for a drop would name it. **Working assumption:** "Pick up sticks" meets a wolf one time in ten; "Lumber from the deep woods" is three trees or four, the first count that is a range, with a wolf or two on half its runs. They are the two jobs that vary so far.

## Locations and camps

- **Decided.** A location is one of a fixed set of places the game defines: villages, cities, strongholds and the like. Every player visits the same locations.
- **Decided.** A player unlocks a location and then has a camp there. A new player starts with one location unlocked and no heroes. A further location opens with an unlock ("Unlocks" below; decision 0059), which the engine and the hub have (built by Issue-351). **Decided, not built (Issue-352):** a job of the caravan hub grants the first, and the page shows the camp it opens. Until then no player can hold an unlock, and the page has one location.
- **Decided (decision 0059). Built by Issue-351, in the engine and the hub: its hall, warehouse, board, job and the miner and the fighter. Not built: Issue-352 for its camp on the page, Issue-356 for its quarry, its stone, its knight and its caravan hub.** The second location is Highcrag, a hill fort above a quarry, supplied from Millbrook and not a second start. Every camp there has the heroes hall and the warehouse, beside the shared job board, and may build a quarry and a caravan hub; until Issue-356 nothing can be built there but the hall's and the warehouse's levels. It recruits the miner, the fighter and the knight, which is found only there, and no villager (the owner, 2026-11-23), no lumberjack and no ranger. So there is no free villager at Highcrag: a camp there begins empty, and its first hero comes by caravan, as a hero who stays or as a cargo that pays for a recruit. Its board has one job, "Ore from the crag", for ore, which a lone miner completes. Its quarry gives stone, a new resource, which a stone outcrop drops to a party that mines at level 2 and to no other: the first drop that needs a level, with no new trait, so a villager brings home none and is not told why. Highcrag has no trees, so what its buildings and its miner cost in timber comes by caravan. Its camp is packed earth on bare rock: the earth runs under every building, and a site the camp works, the quarry or a later one such as a pass, stands on the rock (the owner, 2026-11-24, in the mock-up of Issue-350; drawn by Issue-352). The knight costs ore and hide, the first thing that costs hide. **Working assumption:** at Highcrag a miner costs 30 ore and 20 timber and a fighter 40 ore, as at Millbrook, and the board's job is the mine's first job on another hill, two ore veins and then a wolf, with a bonus of 4 (the owner, 2026-11-24, Issue-351): over 500 seeds a miner, a fighter, two of either, one of each and four miners each complete it with no death, a party with a miner brings home 56 to 64 ore, which pays for a fighter, and a party without one brings home none; an outcrop has 40 health, gives 3 XP and drops 20 to 24 stone at mining 2; the knight's numbers and cost and the quarry's cost and jobs are agreed at the start of Issue-356.
- **Decided.** A camp is one player's view of a location, and it is the main view for a signed-in player. One camp is on screen at a time, and the player switches between their unlocked camps at will with a select in the page's header bar. The header bar is thin and also holds the title, sign-in and the open adventure, so the player can always find it.
- **Decided.** A location has two kinds of facility. **Own facilities** belong to one player, who builds and upgrades them, so two players' camps at the same location can differ in which facilities they have and at what level. **Shared facilities** belong to the location and look the same to every player.
- **Decided.** Facilities are where the player acts: they open a facility by clicking it in the scene or by choosing it from the page's menu ("The page"). Most facilities offer missions, each its own kinds. A mine offers mining missions; a forest path, lumber runs; a caravan hub, caravans. The first is the **job board**, a shared facility offering general jobs, so a new player can set out without building anything.
- **Decided.** A job is posted by the job board or by one facility. A facility's jobs are listed in its panel once the camp has built it, the job board lists only the jobs no facility posts, and the hub refuses to start a facility's job at a camp that has not built that facility (Issue-133).
- **Decided.** Heroes walk about the camp for flavor: some of the player's heroes at that location and some of other players' heroes there, never all of either. Clicking a hero shows who it is. Nothing is done through them. Another player's hero is one seen at the location in the last week, through being recruited there for now and through missions later; its card shows its name, unit type and level, never whose it is (decision 0029).
- **Decided.** A location shows what happened there (2026-11-10, Issue-282, decision 0050). The job board's panel lists the adventures recently completed at the location, the last week's, newest first, up to ten: each with its job, how it ended and its party, never whose it was. A job's page lists that job's recent replays, retreats and defeats included. Any of them opens on a page of its own that plays the replay, with the same pause and speeds as the player's own. The player's own adventures are in both lists like anyone's.
- **Decided.** A camp is a map of whole 16 px tiles, Millbrook's 400×225. The scene shows the map scaled up by the largest whole number of device pixels per art pixel at which its height and its middle, where every facility stands, fit the box; it fills the rest of the box with more ground around the map, or crops the map's bare edges where the box is a little narrow, so there are no bars and no stretching. It never scrolls. It sits below the header bar, which is part of the page and not of the scene (decision 0028, as amended by Issue-89 and Issue-95).
- **Decided.** Heroes in the camp are drawn at half the scene's scale, rounded up to a whole number, so they stand about a third of the hall's height rather than half, and their pixels stay square; never smaller than one CSS pixel per art pixel, so they can still be tapped on a phone. Battle keeps its own scale (decision 0028, as amended by Issue-99 and Issue-95).

## The heroes hall

- **Decided.** Every unlocked location has the player's own heroes hall, at level 1 or above. The hall is where heroes are recruited, and where the player views and manages their heroes at that location.
- **Decided.** A hero belongs to one camp at a time. The hall's level sets the number of active heroes the camp can hold. Living and dead heroes are both active. A retired hero is not: retiring is permanent, and a retired hero stays on the record with its history, like a dead one. A hero on an adventure cannot retire until the adventure is over (owner, 2026-10-22, Issue-113).
- **Decided.** Recruiting anyone needs a free slot. When no active hero at the location is alive, the next villager is free, so a player can never be locked out: a camp full of dead heroes retires one and recruits a villager for nothing. Decision 0059 keeps the rule and narrows what it promises (built by Issue-351): it holds wherever a villager is recruited, which Highcrag does not do, so a camp there whose heroes are all dead waits for a caravan; the player is still never locked out of the game, because Millbrook is every player's.
- **Decided.** Each location lists the unit types that can be recruited there, each with the hall level it needs and its cost in resources. Common unit types are on many lists; some are found in one place only, such as the templar at a temple or the witch in a swamp. The starting location offers the villager at hall level 1, which is what makes the free villager work there, and Highcrag recruits none (decision 0059, built by Issue-351; "Locations and camps"). A recruit names the camp it is made at, and is paid from that camp's stock.
- **Decided.** The first location is Millbrook, a quiet village. **Working assumption:** it recruits the villager, the lumberjack, the miner and the fighter at hall level 1, and the ranger at hall level 2, the first unit type a hall's level gates (Issue-286, decision 0052). The fighter's first level is easy to reach. The specialized gatherers (Issue-287): a lumberjack costs 40 timber, a villagers' timber run's worth and an alternative to saving for the mine; a miner costs 30 ore and 20 timber, an alternative to the fighter after a villagers' ore run. Neither needs its facility built: the miner's jobs are the mine's already, and a lumberjack cuts wood on the board's jobs as it does on the lumber camp's own, "Lumber from the deep woods" (Issue-291; "The battle"). On the timber job a lumberjack brings home 84 to 100 timber where villagers bring 60 to 72; on the ore job a miner brings home 56 to 64 ore where villagers bring 40 to 48: each node's drop at level 2 of its trait against level 1 (decision 0058; "The battle").
- **Decided.** The first progression, each step paid for by one run of the job before it (2026-10-22, Issue-126, decision 0031): the free villager picks up sticks, which pays for a second villager; the villagers' timber run pays for the mine; the mine's first job pays for a fighter, with ore; and with a fighter the wolves job is safe. **Working assumption,** the numbers: a villager costs 20 timber; the mine at level 1, 60 timber and 3 cloth; a fighter, 40 ore. Paid recruitment is Issue-82, the mine Issue-93, the mine's first job Issue-133. One step more (2026-11-11, Issue-286, decision 0052): the wolves job's cloth, with timber, pays for the hall's second level and then for a ranger, 40 timber and 10 cloth, and with a ranger the mine's second job, the goblins in the old workings, is safe.
- **Decided.** A hero moves to another camp only on a caravan, as a hero of its party who is marked to stay at the destination; it fights like any other, where this item first had it "like cargo, possibly as a non-combatant" (decision 0059; not built, Issue-354; "Caravans" below). Its slot is held at both camps for the whole trip: at the start the destination must have a free slot; at settlement the hero lands at the destination if delivered and stays at home if not, and the other slot is released. A passenger who dies on the way arrives dead and counts there.

## Resources

- **Decided.** There is no currency. Everything is paid in resources, such as wood, iron and ore: a miner may cost iron, an archer wood. If gold exists, it is one more resource.
- **Decided (direction).** Many kinds of resource, and many gathering traits to collect them, added a little at a time (the owner, 2026-11-19; decision 0058). The first new one is hide, which a wolf drops to a party that can skin and which nothing costs yet (built by Issue-340); the knight at Highcrag will cost it, decided and not built (Issue-356, decision 0059). The next is stone, at Highcrag, gathered by mining at level 2 with no new trait (Issue-356; "Locations and camps").
- **Decided.** Resources are held per location: each camp has its own stock. They move between locations only by caravan, which is what makes supplying a remote camp a job of its own.
- **Decided.** Each camp's warehouse caps the stock of each resource by its level. Every unlocked location has one at level 1 or above, like the heroes hall. A credit that would pass the cap stops at it and the rest is lost; the result says how much. Before setting out, the player is shown how much room the camp has for the job's pay, and not for what the job may drop, because a job does not say what it drops (decision 0058); the warehouse's panel shows the room for everything.
- **Decided.** A player's own facilities, their levels, their resources and their heroes' private details are owner-only data, which field-level authorization protects.
- **Open.** Which unit types a location beyond Millbrook and Highcrag offers, and the facilities' levels beyond the first ones below. The numbers live in the game's static content as working assumptions. Millbrook's recruitment costs and the mine's first level are set by the first progression ("The heroes hall").
- **Decided.** Building a facility is instant, because nothing in the game gates on real time ("The adventure loop"; 2026-10-22, Issue-126).
- **Decided (direction).** A camp is not meant to build everything at its first location. Higher levels will need resources that other locations have, so the logistics between locations, caravans above all, grow into a larger part of the game (the owner, 2026-10-22, Issue-93). **Decided, not built (Issue-357; decision 0059):** the first such levels are a second level of the mine, the lumber camp and the caravan hub at Millbrook, each costing stone, which only Highcrag has; the hub's gives a larger cart, and what the other two give, and every cost, is agreed at the start of that issue. The costs below do not change.
- **Working assumption.** The levels and costs at first (Issue-93, decision 0032): the heroes hall and the warehouse go to level 3, and the mine, the lumber camp and the caravan hub to level 1 (each gains a second by decision 0059, not built, Issue-357). The hall holds 4, 6 and 8 heroes; the warehouse 100, 200 and 400 of each resource. The hall's level 2 costs 80 timber and 20 cloth, its level 3 150 timber, 40 ore and 30 cloth; the warehouse's level 2 80 timber and 40 ore, its level 3 160 timber, 120 ore and 20 cloth; the mine 60 timber and 3 cloth (the first progression), the lumber camp 50 timber and 20 ore, the caravan hub 100 timber, 60 ore and 20 cloth. Every level-1 cost fits in a level-1 warehouse.
- **Decided.** A facility's level may need another facility first. The first such rule: the caravan hub needs the warehouse at level 2, since caravans carry stock (Issue-93).

## Unlocks

Decided on 2026-11-23 (Issue-292, decision 0059). The engine's and the hub's side is built (Issue-351); the page's, with the first job that grants one, is not (Issue-352), so no player holds an unlock yet.

- **Decided (built by Issue-351).** An unlock is something a player earns once and keeps: one-time, the player's own, and permanent. It is not only for camps. A mission is one way to earn one and a new camp one thing it opens, and there will be other ways and other things, such as an unlock earned by defeating a kind of enemy with a hero that carries a trait (the owner, 2026-11-23).
- **Decided (built by Issue-351).** The game's content lists every unlock, with its name and the words said when it is earned. What needs an unlock names it, a location first; what grants one names it, a job on arrival first. An adventure's result says which unlocks the run earned, so a later way of earning one is the engine's to add. A retreat and a party that is all down earn none. **Working assumption,** the words: the first unlock is "The road to Highcrag", and when it is earned the player is told "The road to Highcrag is open. You have a camp there now."
- **Decided, not built (Issue-352).** An unlock is told when it is earned: the player is told each one the run earned that they did not have. A result is public and what a player holds is not, so the result lists what the run earned, as it lists what dropped, and only the player is told which were new. The page works that out from the player's own unlocks, each of which names the adventure that first earned it: an unlock in a result is new when the player's names that adventure (the owner, 2026-11-24; the hub's side built by Issue-351). Whether a job says beforehand what it grants is its description's choice, as it is for a drop ("The battle", "Not every drop is told"), so an unlock may be a secret.
- **Decided (built by Issue-351).** A location may need an unlock, and the starting location needs none. With it the player has a camp there, its heroes hall and warehouse at level 1 and no heroes. Without it the player has no camp there: nothing can be recruited, built or started at a location the player has not unlocked.
- **Decided, not built (Issue-352).** The first unlock is the road to Highcrag, which "Scout the road to Highcrag" grants, a job of Millbrook's caravan hub: travel, bandits on the road, arrive, with no cart, and the party comes home. So the caravan hub is the one door to the rest of the world, and a camp that is opened can be reached. The job's description says what it opens, and it stays listed afterwards as a job like any other. **Working assumption:** the road's fight is two bandits, the bandit's first job.
- **Decided (built by Issue-351).** A player's unlocks are owner-only data, like their facilities and their resources ("Resources").

## Caravans

Decided on 2026-11-23 (Issue-292, decision 0059) and not built: the cart in the engine is Issue-353, the hub's side Issue-354, and the page Issue-355.

- **Decided, not built (Issue-354).** A caravan is a job that delivers. A route is a job the caravan hub posts, "Caravan to Highcrag", run by a party of one to four like any job. It has no pay: the delivery is the reward.
- **Decided, not built (Issue-353).** The cart is a party member with health and no actions, on the near line. It is hit like any party member and a fighter's guard covers it like any ally. It is not a hero: it earns no XP, carries no trait and does not count among the heroes standing when a drop is read, and a party whose heroes are all down is down whether or not the cart stands. Something on the road aims at it: a bandit goes for the cart first when the cart is in its reach. On a travel the cart has no animation of its own: the scene bobs its still picture while the party walks in place. A cart that goes down leaves its wreck on the road where it fell, and does not fade as a fallen monster does (the owner, 2026-11-24, Issue-350; the pictures are `art/props/cart-01`). **Working assumption:** the cart has 60 health, and a caravan's road is two fights, bandits in each, with a wolf by chance.
- **Decided, not built (Issue-354).** The cargo is chosen at the start from the camp's warehouse, up to what the cart carries, which the caravan hub's level sets. It leaves the warehouse when the caravan departs. **Working assumption:** the cart carries 100 in all at hub level 1, which the owner has seen, and 200 at level 2 (Issue-357), which the owner has not. What a caravan carried is the player's own to see while it is on the road, and once it is over its result says what was delivered and what was lost, to anyone, as every result does.
- **Decided, not built (Issue-354).** Passengers fight. Every hero on a caravan is in its party and acts by its unit type's list; the player marks which of them stay at the destination, and the rest are the escort and come home ("The heroes hall", for the slot held at both ends).
- **Decided, not built (Issue-353 for what the engine says, Issue-354 for what the hub does).** What the end of the run does:

  | The run ends | Cargo | Heroes marked to stay | Drops and the enemies' XP | Bonus |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Arrives, cart standing | credited at the destination up to its warehouse's cap, the rest lost, and the result says how much | land there, a dead one too, and count there | kept | paid |
  | Arrives, cart broken | lost | land there | kept | paid |
  | Retreat, ordered or by a time limit | abandoned | stay home | kept | lost |
  | All down | lost | dead, at home | none | none |
  | Discarded after two updates ("The adventure loop") | returned to the camp it left, up to its cap | stay home | none | none |

  The cargo is all or nothing. A broken cart does not end the run, because the passengers still have somewhere to go.
- **Decided, not built (Issue-354).** Everything but the cargo and the heroes who stay goes to the camp the caravan left, as every job's does: the drops, the enemies' XP and the bonus. The adventure is listed in that camp's "Recently here".
- **Decided, not built (Issue-354, Issue-356).** One direction per hub. Millbrook's hub sends to Highcrag; coming back needs Highcrag's own hub and its job, "Caravan to Millbrook". A hero who stays before that hub is built stays until it is.
- **Decided, not built (Issue-355).** A caravan's page is a job's page with two more picks, the cargo and who stays. It shows the destination's room for each resource of the cargo, where a job's page shows none for a drop, because the cargo is the player's own choice and names nothing hidden, and the destination hall's free slots. Before departing it says that a retreat abandons the cargo and, where the destination has no caravan hub, that a hero who stays cannot come back until it has one. The result says what was delivered, what the destination's cap lost and who stayed. The destination's roster shows a slot held for a hero on its way. A route whose destination the player has not unlocked is listed with the reason.
- **Open.** Whether a caravan costs energy: Issue-337 is not decided, and nothing here is built toward it.
