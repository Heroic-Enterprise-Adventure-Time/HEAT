# The game

What the player does in HEAT. How the system delivers it is in `docs/architecture/overview.md`.

Each item carries a status. **Decided** items are settled by the project owner. **Working assumption** items were proposed during design and not yet objected to: build on them, but expect them to be revisited. **Open** items are not yet answered. An item marked "not built" is decided and names the issue that builds it; until that issue is merged, the items it replaces say what the game does (`docs/README.md`, rule 15).

## The business

- **Decided.** The player runs an adventuring business.
- **Decided.** Heroes are close to unit types: every hero of a unit type starts with the same stats and abilities as every other, and what changes afterwards changes numbers, never abilities (see "Heroes"). Adding a unit type should be simple.
- **Decided (direction, if time allows).** Progression means gaining unit types that get more done with less intervention, automating what the player used to do by hand and freeing them for the next problem.
- **Decided.** A **job** is the template; an **adventure** is one run of a job. The names may change.
- **Decided.** Jobs are more than battles. Examples: take a cart of goods from one settlement to another; go into the forest, chop lumber and return safely; fight through a hundred goblins and kill their king. An adventure progresses linearly, doing things along the way.
- **Working assumption.** An outcome is richer than win or lose: how far the party got, what it gathered or delivered, who was lost.
- **Decided.** The villager is the starting hire: cheap to hire, with no specialized skills. Specialized laborers are unit types of their own: the miner and the lumberjack, recruited at Millbrook (Issue-287, "The heroes hall").

## Heroes

What makes one hero different from another of the same unit type. Nothing here changes a unit type's AI or gives a hero an ability its unit type lacks: every unit type keeps one way of choosing its actions.

- **Decided.** Appearance is cosmetic. At recruitment a hero is given a sprite set, drawn at random from the sets its unit type allows, and a name, drawn at random from a fixed list. A set may serve more than one unit type. The hero keeps both for good.
- **Decided.** The name suits the sprite. Every sprite set and every name is labelled masculine, feminine or either. A masculine set takes a masculine or either name, a feminine set a feminine or either name, and an either set any name. The name is drawn after the set, and avoids the names of the camp's living heroes while any are left. Heroes recruited before the labels keep their names. There is no renaming, so no text a player wrote is ever shown to another player, and the game needs no moderation.
- **Decided.** Advancement. A hero earns XP in adventures, credited at settlement, and XP brings levels. Each level offers a choice between small, fixed stat boosts from the unit type's list, so two heroes of the same unit type and level can differ.
- **Decided (direction).** Advanced classes. A hero may later move to an advanced class, which swaps its unit type and starts it again at level one. A temporary dip in strength is acceptable; an advanced class at its top level beats its base class at its top level.
- **Decided.** Weapons. Each unit type uses one weapon kind and no other: rangers use bows, knights use swords. A weapon changes numbers, never abilities. Weapons are crafted from materials; a rare weapon needs at least one rare material.
- **Working assumption.** A weapon lasts a number of fights, a fight being one battle stage. A hero whose weapon breaks fights on with its unit type's unarmed numbers, so a broken weapon never strands a party.
- **Decided.** Death. A hero can die for good. Bringing a dead hero back always costs a resource or a consumable and needs a facility. Cheaper forms carry a penalty: lost XP, a lost level, or time to recover. The cheapest form comes early in the camp's progression, so losing a villager on a boss run hurts without being frustrating. A dead hero stays on the record with its history.
- **Working assumption.** Consumables. Crafted from materials and taken on an adventure in a small pack chosen at the start, which goes into the adventure's snapshot. Each is used through an override: party-wide heals, shields and bonuses that let a player spend their way through a hard mission. Unused consumables return to the player's holdings at settlement.
- **Decided.** History. Each hero keeps a record of what it has done, such as adventures survived, monsters defeated and goods delivered.
- **Open.** The stat boosts each unit type offers, the XP curve, which advanced classes exist, and the costs of resurrection, weapons and consumables.

## How the game looks

The rules for making a hero's sprite set are `docs/process/assets.md`, rules 8 to 13, for a camp's ground and facilities rules 14 to 19, for the page's icons rules 25 to 27, for the battle's monsters and props rules 28 to 31, and for the weapons and effects of an action rules 32 to 35; this section says what the player sees.

- **Decided.** One view for camp and battle: low top-down, about 20 degrees, the classic 3/4 view of an RPG town. Battle arranges the same sprites so that it reads as side-on. Two pictures are not in that view (Issue-350; `docs/process/assets.md`, rules 14 and 31): a prop that stands on the party's line, the caravan's cart, is drawn from the side, because it has one picture where a hero has a frame that faces east; and a ground made since the service changed is drawn from higher up, which flat ground does not show.
- **Decided.** An NES-era look, with Final Fantasy 1 as the reference: warm, earthy colours, a black outline, few colours per sprite, characters 32 px tall.
- **Decided.** Characters, monsters, environment and UI share one theme. Ranger 01 (`art/heroes/ranger-01/`) is the baseline.
- **Decided.** Characters hold nothing. Every action, whatever the ability, is the hero's act animation, a three-frame lead jab whose middle frame is held for as long as the action lasts, played with two separate sprites: the weapon, which appears beside the hero, and the ability's effect, such as a slash, an arrow or a spell. A weapon's one sprite is also its icon in the inventory. Its motion is drawn as animation frames, not rotated in code, because rotated pixel art blurs at any angle but a right angle.
- **Decided.** A sprite set's animations are walk, stance, act, hit and down. A hero standing outside battle is its still frame; there is no idle animation.
- **Decided.** A camp stands on a 16 px tiled ground with no outline, so that the outlined buildings and heroes stand off it. Each facility is one sprite at a three-quarter angle, front wall facing down-right, the same for every facility. A facility's level may change its picture, never its size or its angle (decision 0027).
- **Decided.** The page's icons, such as a facility's entry in the menu or a resource, and the favicon, are 32 px pixel art in the same look, drawn at a whole-number scale. Hide, timber and ore have theirs, picked from one pack so that they share a style (Issue-340, Issue-344), and the battle scene's picture of a drop was the first thing to draw a resource's icon. Decision 0061 shows them as resource chips, which the camp's panels draw (Issue-386). What the scene says in characters is pixel art too: the digits of a drop's amount are sprites cut from a pixel font, at the scene's scale, not text the browser sets (decision 0058). The favicon is a gold coin with a sword through it: money and a weapon, the business of adventuring (Issue-89).

## The page

How the page around the scene is laid out (Issue-89). The scene's own rules are in "Locations and camps" and decision 0028.

- **Decided.** Four regions, and nothing overlaps. The **header bar**: the title, the camp select, the open adventure and sign-in, nothing more; resources are not shown there, because a camp will hold many kinds. The **facility menu**: an entry with an icon for every facility the camp has or may build, the same list the scene draws. The **stage**: the scene, filling the space the other regions leave it. The **content area**: always one panel, such as the heroes hall, the job board, the warehouse or a hero's card; the heroes hall when nothing else is chosen. Decision 0061 adds a nav fixed to the bottom of the window where the regions stack, with the panel padded by its height so that nothing is hidden under it (built by Issue-386).
- **Decided.** Where there is room, the menu is on the left, the stage in the middle and the content area on the right, beside the scene and never over it. The side columns give way as the window narrows, not only the scene: the content area takes about a quarter of the window, between 17rem and 28rem, and the menu is an icon rail that shows its labels only where the wider menu costs the scene no scale step, with its icons at twice the size beside a scene at scale 3 or more. Where side by side would leave the scene below scale 2, on a tablet, a small window or a phone, the regions stack: the scene at the top, the page's width, then the menu as a row of icons, then the content area, which scrolls with the page. Decision 0061 replaces the row of icons with a nav fixed to the bottom of the window (built by Issue-386).
- **Decided.** Where the player is, is the address's path: the camp, then its panel, `/camp/<location>/<facility>`, with a hero's card under the hall, `/camp/<location>/heroes-hall/heroes/<id>`. The browser's back returns to the last panel, a reload keeps it, a panel can be linked to, and the menu marks the hall while one of its heroes is open. A panel moves from a list to a detail and back, as the hall's roster does to a hero's card. The open adventure is `/adventure`, and any settled adventure's replay `/replays/<id>`, each outside any camp, because the battle takes the stage (Issue-290).
- **Decided.** What was done at a location is listed where it was done (decision 0050). The job board has "Recently here": up to ten of the location's adventures from the last week, newest first, each led by its job, then how it ended, how long ago, and its party, each hero with its unit type, its level and whether it died. A job has "Replays", the same lines led by how long ago. Each line opens the adventure's replay page, which plays it with the pause and speeds of the player's own replay, beside where and when, how it ended, its stages and its party, and says "replay unavailable" for one stored before the battles changed. A list is read when its panel opens, not after each of the player's actions, and a list that cannot be read says so and leaves the rest of the panel working. Decision 0061 moves both lists behind the panel's "more" (⋯) menu, each a panel of its own with a link back (built by Issue-386).
- **Decided.** A facility the camp has not built stands in the scene as an unfinished building, its level 0, and is in the menu like any other. Its panel says what it will do.
- **Decided.** Building is done from the facility's panel (decision 0032). The panel of a facility not built shows what building it costs, each resource beside the camp's balance, and a Build button; the panel of a built one, the heroes hall's and the warehouse's included, its level and its next level the same way, with what the level gives, such as "Holds 6 heroes". The button is disabled with the reason when the camp cannot build it: another facility needed first, or a resource short. A refusal from the hub is shown in place. At its highest level, the panel says so. A build shows at once in the scene, the facility's picture swapped in place.
- **Decided (built by Issue-355).** A caravan's page is a job's page with the cargo and who stays added ("Caravans").
- **Decided.** Resources are shown where they matter, not in a bar: the warehouse lists the camp's stock against its cap, and every cost or reward is weighed against the camp's balance. Decision 0061 shows each as a resource chip (built by Issue-386).
- **Decided.** In battle the content area is reserved for the party and the controls instead of covering the scene, which scales into what is left; on a narrow screen they go below it.
- **Decided (built by Issue-386 for the header, the nav and the camp's panels, and by Issue-390 for the battle pages).** The page reads as a casual mobile game, in the daylight parchment theme, with less text (decision 0061). An action, something that changes the game such as departing, recruiting, building or retiring, is a button, with its icon where it has one. Navigation, going to another panel or page such as back, the open adventure or the heroes hall, is a link that stands on its own and not inside a sentence. A reward, a cost or a holding is a resource chip, a party is hero tiles, and a count of slots, such as a hall's heroes against its room, is slot circles, filled for each slot taken and empty for each one free, named "2 of 4 heroes". A sentence that explains a chip or a tile is cut to what the chip or tile cannot say.
- **Decided (built by Issue-386).** Where the regions stack, on a camp's pages, the scene stays at the top. The menu is a nav fixed to the bottom of the window, with the facilities' icons. Between them one panel scrolls, padded by the nav's height so that nothing is hidden under it. The open adventure and a replay, where the battle takes the stage, have no nav.
- **Decided (built by Issue-386).** A panel's secondary views are behind its own "more" (⋯) menu, a button in the panel's title: the job board's "Recently here" and a job's "Replays" first. The menu shows and hides a short list of links; Escape closes it. Each view is a panel of its own, under the panel it belongs to, `/camp/<location>/job-board/recent` and `…/jobs/<job>/replays`, with a link back to it. A replay is still its own page, `/replays/<id>`. The header bar keeps its four items.
- **Decided (built by Issue-386).** Resources are shown by panel, never all at once. A resource chip is the resource's icon and an amount. A job shows what it pays and what it costs, and the warehouse shows every resource against its cap. A cost is a chip that knows the camp's balance, in place of the balance written beside it: a cost the camp cannot meet is short, in rust with a dashed outline, so that it does not rest on colour alone, and is named "more than the camp has" to a screen reader (owner's call in review of PR-388). A button such a cost disables still says why. This keeps the header bar's reason: a camp will hold many kinds.
- **Decided (built by Issue-386).** A hero tile shows a hero picked, not picked or empty. Only the picked hero has the ink outline, and a hero not picked has a soft tan one. In a pick, such as the party or who stays, a tile is a toggle, and a hero beyond a full party cannot be pressed. In the hall's roster a tile is a link to the hero's card, and a hero on its way by caravan is a tile that is no control.

## The adventure loop

- **Decided.** An adventure runs deterministically in the browser on a timestamped timeline and is submitted in one piece for verification. The game is not offline-first; the aim is to avoid a request per click.
- **Decided.** The timeline's clock counts whole milliseconds. Charge timers and turns run on that integer clock.
- **Decided.** The player can interject actions that may override a hero's next action. An override waits for the hero's next turn and applies if it is allowed at that moment; if it is not, the hero follows its own logic. Overrides go through the seeded run like everything else, so the timeline stays deterministic.
- **Decided.** No real-time gating. Players can pause, and can fast-forward, including through content they have automated.
- **Decided.** One unresolved adventure per player at a time.
  - It must be easy for the player to find, so they know it is in progress and can return to it.
  - If the browser closes, the adventure waits. On next sign-in the app checks for an unresolved adventure and picks it up, replaying the overrides so far with the client's own engine.
  - If the player never returns, it stays unresolved.
  - An adventure survives one game update and not two: the update before the current one stays able to verify it, so a player in the middle of an adventure when an update ships finishes and submits as if nothing happened. After a second update it is discarded, and the player is told (decision 0030, amending 0003).
  - A player who dislikes how a run is going must finish it to continue, or wait for the next update.
- **Decided.** A replay is what the server verified, played back. It is display only: any version of the game plays it, and it never needs the engine again.

## The battle

An adventure is played as a battle that plays itself: each unit acts from its unit type's fixed list on a marching order of four lines, and the player watches, pauses, fast-forwards and interjects. Its stages, units, overrides, retreat, drops, XP and the jobs' numbers are `docs/design/battle.md`.

## Locations and camps

- **Decided.** A location is one of a fixed set of places the game defines: villages, cities, strongholds and the like. Every player visits the same locations.
- **Decided.** A player unlocks a location and then has a camp there. A new player starts with one location unlocked and no heroes. A further location opens with an unlock ("Unlocks" below; decision 0059; built by Issue-351 in the engine and the hub): a job of the caravan hub grants the first, and the page shows the camp it opens (built by Issue-352).
- **Decided (decision 0059). Built by Issue-351, in the engine and the hub: its hall, warehouse, board, job and the miner and the fighter; and its camp on the page. Built by Issue-356: its quarry, its stone, its knight and its caravan hub.** The second location is Highcrag, a hill fort above a quarry, supplied from Millbrook and not a second start. Every camp there has the heroes hall and the warehouse, beside the shared job board, and may build a quarry and a caravan hub. It recruits the miner, the fighter and the knight, which is found only there, and no villager, no lumberjack and no ranger. So there is no free villager at Highcrag: a camp there begins empty, and its first hero comes by caravan, as a hero who stays or as a cargo that pays for a recruit. Its board has one job, "Ore from the crag", for ore, which a lone miner completes. Its quarry gives stone, a new resource, which a stone outcrop drops to a party that mines at level 2 and to no other: the first drop that needs a level, with no new trait, so a villager brings home none and is not told why. Highcrag has no trees, so what its buildings and its miner cost in timber comes by caravan. Its camp is packed earth on bare rock: the earth runs under every building, and a site the camp works, the quarry or a later one such as a pass, stands on the rock; the caravan hub stands on the earth at the foot of the yard and the quarry on the rock to the east. The knight costs ore and hide, the first thing that costs hide. **Working assumption:** at Highcrag a miner costs 30 ore and 20 timber and a fighter 40 ore, as at Millbrook, and the board's job is the mine's first job on another hill, two ore veins and then a wolf, with a bonus of 4: over 500 seeds a miner, a fighter, two of either, one of each and four miners each complete it with no death, a party with a miner brings home 56 to 64 ore, which pays for a fighter, and a party without one brings home none; an outcrop has 40 health, gives 3 XP and drops 20 to 24 stone at mining 2; the knight stands on the front line with 70 health, speed 8 and damage 7, guards and strikes, and costs 50 ore and 4 hide; the quarry costs 60 timber and 40 ore and posts "Stone from the quarry face", two or three outcrops and a wolf on half the runs, which every party the probe played there completes with no death, a lone miner, fighter or knight and the pairs and threes of decision 0059's amendment, and "Goblins in the quarry", three outcrops and a goblin rock thrower, which defeats any hero alone and which a miner with a knight clears with no death (decision 0059, its amendment of Issue-356).
- **Decided.** A camp is one player's view of a location, and it is the main view for a signed-in player. One camp is on screen at a time, and the player switches between their unlocked camps at will with a select in the page's header bar, which lists a location once the player has a camp there; the address of a camp the player does not have goes to the starting camp (decision 0059). The header bar is thin and also holds the title, sign-in and the open adventure, so the player can always find it.
- **Decided.** A location has two kinds of facility. **Own facilities** belong to one player, who builds and upgrades them, so two players' camps at the same location can differ in which facilities they have and at what level. **Shared facilities** belong to the location and look the same to every player.
- **Decided.** Facilities are where the player acts: they open a facility by clicking it in the scene or by choosing it from the page's menu ("The page"). Most facilities offer missions, each its own kinds. A mine offers mining missions; a forest path, lumber runs; a caravan hub, caravans. The first is the **job board**, a shared facility offering general jobs, so a new player can set out without building anything.
- **Decided.** A job is posted by the job board or by one facility. A facility's jobs are listed in its panel once the camp has built it, the job board lists only the jobs no facility posts, and the hub refuses to start a facility's job at a camp that has not built that facility (Issue-133).
- **Decided.** Heroes walk about the camp for flavor: some of the player's heroes at that location and some of other players' heroes there, never all of either. Clicking a hero shows who it is. Nothing is done through them. Another player's hero is one seen at the location in the last week, through being recruited there for now and through missions later; its card shows its name, unit type and level, never whose it is (decision 0029).
- **Decided.** A location shows what happened there (decision 0050). The job board's panel lists the adventures recently completed at the location, the last week's, newest first, up to ten: each with its job, how it ended and its party, never whose it was. A job's page lists that job's recent replays, retreats and defeats included. Any of them opens on a page of its own that plays the replay, with the same pause and speeds as the player's own. The player's own adventures are in both lists like anyone's.
- **Decided.** A camp is a map of whole 16 px tiles, Millbrook's and Highcrag's each 400×225. The scene shows the map scaled up by the largest whole number of device pixels per art pixel at which its height and its middle, where every facility stands, fit the box; it fills the rest of the box with more ground around the map, or crops the map's bare edges where the box is a little narrow, so there are no bars and no stretching. It never scrolls. It sits below the header bar, which is part of the page and not of the scene (decision 0028).
- **Decided.** Heroes in the camp are drawn at half the scene's scale, rounded up to a whole number, so they stand about a third of the hall's height rather than half, and their pixels stay square; never smaller than one CSS pixel per art pixel, so they can still be tapped on a phone. Battle keeps its own scale (decision 0028).

## The heroes hall

- **Decided.** Every unlocked location has the player's own heroes hall, at level 1 or above. The hall is where heroes are recruited, and where the player views and manages their heroes at that location.
- **Decided.** A hero belongs to one camp at a time. The hall's level sets the number of active heroes the camp can hold. Living and dead heroes are both active. A retired hero is not: retiring is permanent, and a retired hero stays on the record with its history, like a dead one. A hero on an adventure cannot retire until the adventure is over (Issue-113).
- **Decided.** Recruiting anyone needs a free slot. When no active hero at the location is alive, the next villager is free, so a player can never be locked out: a camp full of dead heroes retires one and recruits a villager for nothing. Decision 0059 keeps the rule and narrows what it promises (built by Issue-351): it holds wherever a villager is recruited, which Highcrag does not do, so a camp there whose heroes are all dead waits for a caravan; the player is still never locked out of the game, because Millbrook is every player's. The hall of such a camp says so while nobody is alive there: that nobody joins for nothing, and that a hero comes by caravan from another camp (built by Issue-352).
- **Decided.** Each location lists the unit types that can be recruited there, each with the hall level it needs and its cost in resources. Common unit types are on many lists; some are found in one place only, such as the templar at a temple or the witch in a swamp. The starting location offers the villager at hall level 1, which is what makes the free villager work there, and Highcrag recruits none (decision 0059, built by Issue-351; "Locations and camps"). A recruit names the camp it is made at, and is paid from that camp's stock.
- **Decided.** The first location is Millbrook, a quiet village. **Working assumption:** it recruits the villager, the lumberjack, the miner and the fighter at hall level 1, and the ranger at hall level 2, the first unit type a hall's level gates (decision 0052). The fighter's first level is easy to reach. The specialized gatherers (Issue-287): a lumberjack costs 40 timber, a villagers' timber run's worth and an alternative to saving for the mine; a miner costs 30 ore and 20 timber, an alternative to the fighter after a villagers' ore run. Neither needs its facility built: the miner's jobs are the mine's already, and a lumberjack cuts wood on the board's jobs as it does on the lumber camp's own, "Lumber from the deep woods" (decision 0058; `docs/design/battle.md`, "Millbrook's board"). On the timber job a lumberjack brings home 84 to 100 timber where villagers bring 60 to 72; on the ore job a miner brings home 56 to 64 ore where villagers bring 40 to 48: each node's drop at level 2 of its trait against level 1 (decision 0058; `docs/design/battle.md`, "The traits").
- **Decided.** The first progression, each step paid for by one run of the job before it (decision 0031): the free villager picks up sticks, which pays for a second villager; the villagers' timber run pays for the mine; the mine's first job pays for a fighter, with ore; and with a fighter the wolves job is safe. **Working assumption,** the numbers: a villager costs 20 timber; the mine at level 1, 60 timber and 3 cloth; a fighter, 40 ore. One step more (decision 0052): the wolves job's cloth, with timber, pays for the hall's second level and then for a ranger, 40 timber and 10 cloth, and with a ranger the mine's second job, the goblins in the old workings, is safe.
- **Decided.** A hero moves to another camp only on a caravan, as a hero of its party who is marked to stay at the destination; it fights like any other (decision 0059; built by Issue-354; "Caravans" below). Its slot is held at both camps for the whole trip: at the start the destination must have a free slot; at settlement the hero lands at the destination if delivered and stays at home if not, and the other slot is released. A passenger who dies on the way arrives dead and counts there.

## Resources

- **Decided.** There is no currency. Everything is paid in resources, such as wood, iron and ore: a miner may cost iron, an archer wood. If gold exists, it is one more resource.
- **Decided (direction).** Many kinds of resource, and many gathering traits to collect them, added a little at a time (decision 0058). The first new one is hide, which a wolf drops to a party that can skin (built by Issue-340), and which the knight at Highcrag costs (built by Issue-356, decision 0059). The next is stone, at Highcrag, gathered by mining at level 2 with no new trait (built by Issue-356; "Locations and camps").
- **Decided.** Resources are held per location: each camp has its own stock. They move between locations only by caravan, which is what makes supplying a remote camp a job of its own.
- **Decided.** Each camp's warehouse caps the stock of each resource by its level. Every unlocked location has one at level 1 or above, like the heroes hall. A credit that would pass the cap stops at it and the rest is lost; the result says how much. Before setting out, the player is shown how much room the camp has for the job's pay, and not for what the job may drop, because a job does not say what it drops (decision 0058); the warehouse's panel shows the room for everything.
- **Decided.** A player's own facilities, their levels, their resources and their heroes' private details are owner-only data, which field-level authorization protects.
- **Open.** Which unit types a location beyond Millbrook and Highcrag offers, and the facilities' levels beyond the first ones below. The numbers live in the game's static content as working assumptions. Millbrook's recruitment costs and the mine's first level are set by the first progression ("The heroes hall").
- **Decided.** Building a facility is instant, because nothing in the game gates on real time ("The adventure loop"; decision 0031).
- **Decided (direction).** A camp is not meant to build everything at its first location. Higher levels will need resources that other locations have, so the logistics between locations, caravans above all, grow into a larger part of the game (Issue-93). **Decided (built by Issue-357; decision 0059, its amendment of Issue-357):** the first such levels are a second level of the mine, the lumber camp and the caravan hub at Millbrook, each costing stone, which only Highcrag has. The hub's gives the cart of 200 ("Caravans"); the mine's and the lumber camp's each post a job of their own, "The deep seam" and "The old-growth stand", which a camp whose facility is at level 1 sees listed greyed out with the level it needs, as a caravan not unlocked is, and cannot start. The first levels' costs do not change.
- **Working assumption.** The levels and costs at first (decision 0032): the heroes hall and the warehouse go to level 3, and the mine, the lumber camp and the caravan hub to level 2 (the second by decision 0059, its amendment of Issue-357), and the quarry, at Highcrag, to level 1 (decision 0059, its amendment of Issue-356). The hall holds 4, 6 and 8 heroes; the warehouse 100, 200 and 400 of each resource. The hall's level 2 costs 80 timber and 20 cloth, its level 3 150 timber, 40 ore and 30 cloth; the warehouse's level 2 80 timber and 40 ore, its level 3 160 timber, 120 ore and 20 cloth; the mine 60 timber and 3 cloth (the first progression), the lumber camp 50 timber and 20 ore, the caravan hub 100 timber, 60 ore and 20 cloth, the quarry 60 timber and 40 ore. Every level-1 cost fits in a level-1 warehouse. The second levels cost stone: the mine's 100 timber and 40 stone, the lumber camp's 60 timber, 40 ore and 40 stone, the caravan hub's 150 timber, 80 ore and 80 stone, which needs the warehouse at level 2 as its first level does. A facility's levels are the same wherever it is built, so Highcrag's caravan hub has a second level too (decision 0059, its amendment of Issue-357).
- **Decided.** A facility's level may need another facility first. The first such rule: the caravan hub needs the warehouse at level 2, since caravans carry stock (decision 0032).

## Unlocks

Decided (decision 0059). The engine's and the hub's side is built, and so are the page's and the first job that grants one.

- **Decided (built by Issue-351).** An unlock is something a player earns once and keeps: one-time, the player's own, and permanent. It is not only for camps. A mission is one way to earn one and a new camp one thing it opens, and there will be other ways and other things, such as an unlock earned by defeating a kind of enemy with a hero that carries a trait.
- **Decided (built by Issue-351).** The game's content lists every unlock, with its name and the words said when it is earned. What needs an unlock names it, a location first; what grants one names it, a job on arrival first. An adventure's result says which unlocks the run earned, so a later way of earning one is the engine's to add. A retreat and a party that is all down earn none. **Working assumption,** the words: the first unlock is "The road to Highcrag", and when it is earned the player is told "The road to Highcrag is open. You have a camp there now."
- **Decided (built by Issue-352, the hub's side by Issue-351).** An unlock is told when it is earned: the player is told each one the run earned that they did not have, on the result of that run, under how it ended; where the page could not read the player again after the run was sent, it says so and offers the read again. A replay's page, which anyone may open, says nothing of unlocks. A result is public and what a player holds is not, so the result lists what the run earned, as it lists what dropped, and only the player is told which were new. The page works that out from the player's own unlocks, each of which names the adventure that first earned it: an unlock in a result is new when the player's names that adventure. Whether a job says beforehand what it grants is its description's choice, as it is for a drop (`docs/design/battle.md`, "Not every drop is told"), so an unlock may be a secret.
- **Decided (built by Issue-351).** A location may need an unlock, and the starting location needs none. With it the player has a camp there, its heroes hall and warehouse at level 1 and no heroes. Without it the player has no camp there: nothing can be recruited, built or started at a location the player has not unlocked.
- **Decided (built by Issue-352).** The first unlock is the road to Highcrag, which "Scout the road to Highcrag" grants, a job of Millbrook's caravan hub: travel, bandits on the road, arrive, with no cart, and the party comes home. So the caravan hub is the one door to the rest of the world, and a camp that is opened can be reached. The job's description says what it opens, and it stays listed afterwards as a job like any other. **Working assumption:** the road's fight is two bandits, the bandit's first job, with no pay and a bonus of 8 (`docs/design/battle.md`, "Millbrook's board"); the job's description, the words of the unlock and the words of an empty hall are the page's first, to be changed by looking.
- **Decided (built by Issue-351).** A player's unlocks are owner-only data, like their facilities and their resources ("Resources").

## Caravans

Decided (decision 0059). The cart in the engine and the battle scene is built by Issue-353, the hub's side by Issue-354 and the page by Issue-355.

- **Decided (built by Issue-354).** A caravan is a job that delivers. A route is a job the caravan hub posts, "Caravan to Highcrag", run by a party of one to four like any job. It has no pay: the delivery is the reward.
- **Decided (built by Issue-353).** The cart is a party member with health and no actions, on the near line. It is hit like any party member and a fighter's guard covers it like any ally. It is not a hero: it earns no XP, carries no trait and does not count among the heroes standing when a drop is read, and a party whose heroes are all down is down whether or not the cart stands. Something on the road aims at it: a bandit goes for the cart first when the cart is in its reach. On a travel the cart has no animation of its own: the scene bobs its still picture while the party walks in place. A cart that goes down leaves its wreck on the road where it fell, and does not fade as a fallen monster does (decision 0059; the pictures are `art/props/cart-01`). **Working assumption:** the cart has 60 health, and a caravan's road is two fights, bandits in each, with a wolf by chance. Played over 500 seeds, two bandits a fight with the cart's health carried between them defeat a fighter with a villager on all but one, and one bandit a fight lets every party with a fighter deliver (decision 0059, the amendment of Issue-353); the road Issue-354 put in the content is one bandit a fight, with a wolf on half the seeds, and the cart's 60 health is the owner's to judge.
- **Decided (built by Issue-354).** The cargo is chosen at the start from the camp's warehouse, up to what the cart carries, which the caravan hub's level sets. It leaves the warehouse when the caravan departs. **Working assumption:** the cart carries 100 in all at hub level 1, which the owner has seen, and 200 at level 2, built by Issue-357, which the owner has not. What a caravan carried is the player's own to see while it is on the road, and once it is over its result says what was delivered and what was lost, to anyone, as every result does.
- **Decided (built by Issue-354).** Passengers fight. Every hero on a caravan is in its party and acts by its unit type's list; the player marks which of them stay at the destination, and the rest are the escort and come home ("The heroes hall", for the slot held at both ends).
- **Decided: what the engine says is built by Issue-353; what the hub does by Issue-354.** What the end of the run does:

  | The run ends | Cargo | Heroes marked to stay | Drops and the enemies' XP | Bonus |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Arrives, cart standing | credited at the destination up to its warehouse's cap, the rest lost, and the result says how much | land there, a dead one too, and count there | kept | paid |
  | Arrives, cart broken | lost | land there | kept | paid |
  | Retreat, ordered or by a time limit | abandoned | stay home | kept | lost |
  | All down | lost | dead, at home | none | none |
  | Discarded after two updates ("The adventure loop") | returned to the camp it left, up to its cap | stay home | none | none |

  The cargo is all or nothing. A broken cart does not end the run, because the passengers still have somewhere to go.
- **Decided (built by Issue-354).** Everything but the cargo and the heroes who stay goes to the camp the caravan left, as every job's does: the drops, the enemies' XP and the bonus. The adventure is listed in that camp's "Recently here".
- **Decided (Millbrook's built by Issue-354; Highcrag's by Issue-356).** One direction per hub. Millbrook's hub sends to Highcrag; coming back needs Highcrag's own hub and its job, "Caravan to Millbrook". A hero who stays before that hub is built stays until it is.
- **Decided (built by Issue-355).** A caravan's page is a job's page with two more picks, the cargo and who stays. The cargo is an amount of each resource the camp holds, beside the camp's balance and the destination's room for it, under a line that says the load against what the cart carries. Each amount is set by steps, −100, −10 and −1 below it and +1, +10 and +100 above it, and a step goes no lower than 0 and no higher than what the camp holds or the cart has left, so a camp holding 40 that steps up by 100 loads 40 (the owner, Issue-386). The room is shown here, where a job's page shows none for a drop, because the cargo is the player's own choice and names nothing hidden; a cargo over it is not refused, and the result says what the cap lost. Who stays is a hero tile for each hero of the party, picked or not, beside the destination hall's free slots. A caravan with no cargo and nobody staying is an escort, and departs like any other. Before departing the page says what cannot be undone: the retreat's line names the cargo among what a retreat loses, and, where the destination's camp has no caravan hub, a line says that a hero who stays cannot come back until it has one. Depart waits, with the reason, while more stay than the hall has room for, or while an amount is more than the camp holds or the load more than the cart carries, which the steps never pick but a camp read again may leave; a refusal from the hub, a balance or a slot another start took since the page read the camp, is shown in place. The result says what was delivered and what the destination's cap lost; that the cart broke and its cargo was lost; that the cargo was abandoned in a retreat or lost with a party all down; or, for a caravan discarded because no deploy could verify it, that the cargo went back to the camp it left. It says who stayed, or that nobody did because the caravan did not arrive. The destination's hall lists the heroes on their way under "On the way", each holding a slot its count includes, so a hall that looks to have room and refuses a recruit shows why. A route whose destination the player has not unlocked is listed with what it needs, "Needs the road to Highcrag.", in place of its pay, and its page says so in place of the picks; the words name the unlock and not what grants it, since that is the granting job's description's to say ("Unlocks"). **Working assumption:** the page's words, to be changed by looking.
- **Open.** Whether a caravan costs energy: Issue-337 is not decided, and nothing here is built toward it.
