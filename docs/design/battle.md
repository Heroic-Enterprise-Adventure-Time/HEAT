# The battle

How an adventure plays out, from its stages to what it yields. The rest of the game is `docs/design/game.md`, and how the system delivers it is in `docs/architecture/overview.md`.

Each item carries a status. **Decided** items are settled by the project owner. **Working assumption** items were proposed during design and not yet objected to: build on them, but expect them to be revisited. **Open** items are not yet answered. An item marked "not built" is decided and names the issue that builds it; until that issue is merged, the items it replaces say what the game does (`docs/README.md`, rule 15).

The first cut is decision 0030, with resource nodes reworked by decision 0051, ranged units added by decision 0052, and what an adventure yields decided again by decision 0058. Drops by trait, enemy XP and the one retreat rule are built, so are hide and skinning, the first new pair, and so are jobs that vary, with the lumber camp's first job the first one written for that shape. The shape is decided; the numbers are working assumptions in the engine's static content and are expected to change as the game is played.

- **Decided.** A job is an ordered list of stages: travel, fight, gather and arrive. An adventure runs them on one timeline, with health, what has dropped and the enemies' XP carried between stages. A fight or a gather lists what stands on the other side, monsters and resource nodes together, and ends when all of it is down (decision 0051). A tree is a resource node that drops timber when it is felled, and an ore vein one that drops ore when it is broken (decision 0058). A cart is a party member with health and no actions, for caravans: decided, not built (Issue-353; `docs/design/game.md`, "Caravans"). The stages are the engine's: the page does not list them ("Not every drop is told" below).
- **Decided.** Active time. Each unit has a charge timer that fills at its speed. When it fills, the unit picks the first usable action from its unit type's list, with the target the ability's rule chooses, warms up, resolves, and the timer resets. Nothing waits for the player: the battle plays itself, and the player watches, pauses, fast-forwards and interjects.
- **Decided.** One action list per unit type, fixed. The list is the unit's whole intelligence, and a better unit type is a better list, which is how progression automates what the player used to do by hand (`docs/design/game.md`, "The business"). The player never edits a list.
- **Decided.** A marching order, not a grid. Each side has four lines, front, near, far and back; a line holds any number of units, with no order within it. A unit type has a home line, where it starts; the party pick places nobody. Reach is by line: an ability says which enemy lines it reaches from which of its own. When a side's front line empties, every unit on that side moves up a line, so the nearest occupied line is always the front. Stepping up is also an action, for the unit types whose list says so.
- **Working assumption.** The first unit types and monsters. Not very tactical, by design: a villager walks up and swings, because the player is not meant to keep villagers for long. Decision 0059 puts a caravan's cart first in the bandit's list, when the cart is in its reach: decided, not built (Issue-353; `docs/design/game.md`, "Caravans").

  | Unit | Home line | Its list, first usable action wins |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Villager | near | strike the closest in reach; chop a tree if one is there; mine an ore vein if one is there; move up |
  | Miner | near | mine an ore vein if one is there; chop a tree if one is there; strike the closest in reach; move up |
  | Lumberjack | near | chop a tree if one is there; mine an ore vein if one is there; strike the closest in reach; move up |
  | Fighter | front | guard an ally below half health, if not already guarding; strike the closest |
  | Ranger | far | shoot the closest, on any line |
  | Wolf | front | bite the closest; move up |
  | Bandit | near | strike the weakest in reach; move up |
  | Goblin rock thrower | far | throw a rock at the closest, on any line |

- **Decided.** Ranged units (decision 0052). A ranged attack reaches every line of the other side from every line of its own, where melee reaches only the front. So nodes hold the other side's front line and goblins stand behind them, throwing rocks at the heroes who chip at the nodes, out of reach until the nodes fall and their side moves up, unless the party brings an archer or two to deal with them. A ranger shoots monsters before nodes, like any unit; with nothing else left it shoots a node, which costs the party nothing, because no hit gathers anything and a node drops by the traits of the heroes standing when it falls (decision 0058). **Working assumption,** the numbers: a ranger has 26 health, speed 10 and damage 6, and its shot warms up for 700 ms; a goblin rock thrower has 12 health, speed 8 and damage 5, and its throw warms up for 600 ms.

- **Decided.** A guard lasts a few seconds. While it holds, hits aimed at the guarded ally land on the guard instead, and the guard does not start another until it expires (Issue-110).
- **Decided.** Resource nodes, trees and ore veins, are obstacles (decision 0051). A node stands on the other side's lines like a monster and never acts. It has health, and any hit does its normal damage to it; no hit gathers anything, and what a node gives is its drops, when it goes down ("Drops by trait" below; decision 0058). A regular attack is aimed at a monster first and at a node only when nothing else is in reach. Chop and mine stay as abilities for their look, each a strike at a node of its kind with a strike's damage and warmup, whoever swings. A node is worked only when no monster is in the ability's reach: with a wolf beside a tree a chop has no target, in a unit's list and forced alike, so nobody turns its back on what is biting it (decision 0058). A gathering ability works the node with the least left, so a gatherer fells one tree before starting the next, rather than spreading its work over every tree in reach (Issue-15).
- **Working assumption.** A unit's stats are health, speed (turns per ten seconds), damage and its gathering traits, each with a level ("The traits" below). The first numbers: a villager has 30 health, speed 8 and damage 4, and a miner and a lumberjack have the same (Issue-287); a fighter has 50 health, speed 10 and damage 6; a tree and an ore vein have 40 health each. A specialist works its own material first: its list has its own gathering ability before the other and before a strike. With a monster in reach it strikes that first, as a villager does, since a node is not worked then. On the goblins' mixed stage that costs nothing, because the goblins are out of a melee hero's reach until the veins fall: a specialist in a villager's place loses where villagers lose and wins where they win, with no more deaths. The numbers are in the engine's static content.
- **Decided.** Two kinds of override: forcing a unit's next action, which applies at its next turn if it is usable then and is dropped silently if not; and party orders, press on and retreat. Press on is the default. Retreat can be selected at any time and stays selected. During a fight it is attempted; when a stage completes with it selected, the party retreats for certain (Issue-110; built).
- **Decided.** Retreat. A retreat keeps what dropped and the XP of the enemies that went down, and loses the job's pay and its bonus: one rule for every job (decision 0058; built by Issue-339). No job states a penalty of its own, a caravan included: its cargo is not a drop, and a retreat abandons it (decision 0059; not built, Issue-354; `docs/design/game.md`, "Caravans").
- **Working assumption.** A retreat is attempted every so often until it succeeds, each attempt rolled with the adventure's seeded random number generator. Bonuses and items raise the chance, and the chance rises with each failed attempt, so bad luck alone cannot trap a party. The first numbers: an attempt every 2 s from the order, 30% at first and 20 points more after each failure, so the fifth is certain.
- **Decided.** Every fight and gather stage carries its own time limit, on the adventure's own clock, because some are expected to resolve faster than others. Reaching it ends the adventure as a retreat, which keeps what the retreat rule above keeps. It is a deadlock kill switch, not a mechanic: the timer is shown only in its last ten seconds of simulated time, as a countdown in whole seconds, and nothing is designed to be beaten against it (decision 0030).
- **Decided.** Mana and status effects are left out until the loop works.
- **Decided.** The first job cannot be failed by a new player's free villager alone, whatever the player forces it to do (decision 0031). "Pick up sticks": travel, fell one tree, arrive. It keeps a time limit like every gather stage, set far beyond what the job takes. It brings home timber and no pay, and the timber pays for the next villager. One time in ten a wolf is beside the tree ("A job's adventures vary" below): the villager strikes the wolf first and beats it with a little health left, and no forced action changes that, because a node is not worked while a monster is in reach (decision 0058).
- **Working assumption.** Millbrook's board, easiest first (decisions 0051 and 0058). "Pick up sticks": one tree, which drops a villager 30 to 36 timber, and 5 XP; one time in ten a wolf beside it, for 5 XP more, 10 in all, which a lone hero of any type the camp recruits beats on every seed tried, and which leaves a ranger a hide about half the time. "Timber from the near woods": travel, fell two trees, fight a wolf on the way back, arrive; what the trees drop and a small pay. "Wolves on the mill road": travel, fight two wolves, arrive; paid in cloth. A party is one to four heroes. A job's pay is a reward from whoever posted it, so it need not be loot. The first numbers: the timber job drops villagers 60 to 72 timber and pays 3 cloth and 15 XP; the wolves job pays 10 cloth and 20 XP. A lone villager completes the first two on every seed tried and dies on the third; a fighter with a villager, or three villagers, clear it. A party with a ranger brings hide home from it too, which no job says: a fighter with a ranger completes it on every seed with no death, with no hide on about one run in four, one on half and two on a quarter; the timber job and the ore job each end in one wolf, and give a ranger one hide on about half their runs. The board lists each job's pay and its bonus XP, and never what it brings home ("Not every drop is told" below). The mine posts its own jobs (Issue-133): "Ore from the hill", travel, dig out two ore veins, fight a wolf on the way back, arrive; it drops villagers 40 to 48 ore and gives 15 XP, with no pay. One or two villagers complete it on every seed tried; a fighter with a villager completes it too and brings home the same ore, since one carrier of a trait is enough, and a fighter alone completes it with none. The mine's second job is the first with a mixed stage (decision 0052): "Goblins in the old workings", travel, dig out three ore veins with two goblin rock throwers behind them, arrive; 25 XP, with no pay. One to three villagers are defeated on every seed tried, and so is a fighter with one villager; four villagers complete it with 60 to 72 ore and lose one of them on most runs; a ranger with two villagers completes it on every seed with no death and the same 60 to 72 ore, and with a miner in one villager's place 84 to 96. A ranger with one villager completes it too, and on the few seeds where the villager falls before a vein does it brings home no ore: the carrier is worth protecting. The lumber camp posts its own job too, the first written for decision 0058's shape and not converted to it: "Lumber from the deep woods", travel, fell three trees or four with a wolf or two among them on half its runs, arrive; no pay, and 15 XP at its least roll and 28 at its largest. It drops villagers 90 to 144 timber and a lumberjack 126 to 200, so its worst roll beats the timber job's best. A lone hero of any type the camp recruits completes it with no wolf or one; two wolves, about one run in four, defeat a lone villager, lumberjack, miner or ranger on every seed tried and a lone fighter on about half of them. Two villagers lose one of their number on about one run in ten. Three villagers, a fighter with a villager, and a lumberjack with a fighter complete it on every seed with no death; a lumberjack with a fighter and a ranger does too, with the same timber and a hide from a little under half the wolves it meets, the party that covers every drop. A lumberjack with a ranger alone is the worst pair: on the runs where the lumberjack falls and the ranger finishes, the trees that came down after drop nothing. Its description tells of the trees and of cutters who did not come back, and names neither the wolves nor their hides. The caravan hub posts a job too (decision 0059; `docs/design/game.md`, "Unlocks"): "Scout the road to Highcrag", travel, fight two bandits, arrive; no pay and 20 XP, the bandit's first job and a step past the wolves job. No lone hero of a type Millbrook recruits comes back from it, and neither do two villagers; a fighter with a villager opens the road on every seed and loses one of the two on about one run in four, a fighter with a ranger loses the fighter on about one in eight, and four villagers lose one of their number on most; two fighters, a fighter with a ranger and a villager, and a fighter with three villagers open it with nobody lost. Its description says what it opens, and that one sword is not enough. Each job was played over 500 seeds per party.
- **Decided.** What an adventure yields (decisions 0030 and 0058; built by Issue-339). Arriving pays the job's pay and what dropped, and to each surviving hero the XP of every enemy that went down and the job's bonus. A retreat, ordered or forced by a time limit, keeps what dropped and the enemies' XP and loses the pay and the bonus. A party that is all down keeps nothing. A hero sets out at full health, health carries from stage to stage, and a hero that falls sits out the rest of the job and is dead at its end. A fight or a gather ends when everything on the other side is down, and nothing limits what a party carries (decision 0051).
- **Decided.** An action shows its two sprites (decision 0028), and all of it plays as the action resolves (decision 0057). The unit's weapon appears in its leading hand and swings with the act; at the blow the ability's effect plays on whoever it reached, a slash for a strike, chips of wood for a chop, and the target is hit; the rest of the swing follows. Nothing of an action is drawn while it warms up: the unit stands as it stood until its action happens. The weapon goes by unit type for now, a sword for the fighter, a hand axe for the villager, a dagger for the bandit, and the wolf bites with nothing in hand; a hero's crafted weapon replaces it when equipment exists. A projectile is the target's effect flying from the actor to the target (decision 0052), and its flight is part of its action: the ranger's bow is drawn with the arrow on its string and looses it, the goblin's rock leaves its hand at the top of the throw, and where each lands its sparks or its grit play and the target is hit. The ranger shoots with a bow, and the goblin throws with nothing in hand.
- **Decided.** The battle reads left to right (decision 0028). The party runs on from the left and walks in place while the road scrolls by; at a fight or a gather the other side rushes on from the right as the road scrolls with it, so the party seems to rush to meet it. The rush is flavor: the adventure's clock waits until everyone is on, and only then does the fight begin. The road is drawn on the ground of the camp the party left, the meadow's at Millbrook and the crag's at Highcrag.
- **Decided.** The clock pauses while an action is animated (decision 0057). An action is one instant of the adventure's clock, and when it resolves that clock pauses while the whole of it is shown in one piece: the wind-up, the blow or the release, a projectile's flight, the ability's effect, the target's hit and a fall. One action is seen from its first frame to its last before the next thing happens, so the animations do not run over each other, and nothing in an animation stops while the clock stands. The events of one frame pause the clock once, for the longest of what they begin and not for the sum, and an entry in the same frame takes the pause's place. The pause is the page's, like the rush in. It is measured in the time the page shows, the wall clock times the speed, so it shortens at 2× and 4× as the animations do, and it does not run down while the player has paused. The engine's clock knows nothing of it: a stage's time limit and its countdown, the override log, verification and a stored replay are what they were, and a replay pauses as a run does. **Working assumption:** the pause is the whole of the animation, one share for every kind of clip, a number beside the scene's other timings, and a shot's wind-up and flight together last as long as its warmup, as decision 0052 timed them, 450 ms and 250 ms for the bow and its arrow, 270 ms and 330 ms for the throw and its rock; both are set by looking.
- **Decided.** Drops by trait (decision 0058; built by Issue-339). An enemy, a monster or a resource node, lists zero or more drops, each a resource and the gathering trait that collects it: a tree gives timber to a party that can cut wood, a wolf a hide to a party that can skin. A trait has a level. Who hit what does not matter: when an enemy goes down, the highest level of the drop's trait among the heroes standing in that stage is read, and the drop is rolled for that level, its chance and its amount. A hero that is down does not count, and with no carrier there is no drop. So a fighter is no longer wasted on a resource run and one carrier of a trait is enough: the puzzle is to cover every drop that may show up with a party that still survives the fight. The roll is the enemy's own, from the adventure's seed, and nothing the player does in the fight changes it.
- **Decided.** The traits are named for the craft: woodcutting, mining and skinning. A trait is the unit type's, a number like its other stats. The villager has woodcutting 1 and mining 1, the lumberjack woodcutting 2 and the miner mining 2 (built by Issue-339). The ranger has skinning 1 and the villager cannot skin, so hide is the first drop a party gets nothing of without its carrier (built by Issue-340): a party with no ranger standing when a wolf falls brings home no hide, and is not told why. A specialist carries its own trait alone to start with, so a miner brings home no timber and a lumberjack no ore; a unit type added later may carry several, some at higher levels, as one of the ways it is made interesting (decision 0058). **Working assumption,** the first numbers: a tree drops 30 to 36 timber at woodcutting 1 and 42 to 50 at level 2, an ore vein 20 to 24 ore at mining 1 and 28 to 32 at level 2, each always; a wolf drops one hide half the time at skinning 1, and one or two, always, at level 2, which no unit type has yet. At its worst roll each step of the first progression is still paid by one run of the job before it.
- **Decided.** Not every drop is told (decision 0058; built by Issue-339). A job's description says what its author chooses: "Pick up sticks" says that timber is collected and that a hero who can cut wood is needed, and other jobs may say much less: no job says that a wolf leaves a hide, or who can take it. The job board and a job's page say the description, the pay and the bonus XP, and neither a yield nor the job's stages: travel adds nothing, an arrival is not a stage of its own, and a fight and a gather run alike, so the description is where a job says as much or as little as its author decides. While an adventure is played the page names no stage either. The result says what dropped. A job's page shows the camp's balance and the warehouse's room for the pay alone, since the room for a drop would name it. The rest is the player's to find. Nothing protects it beyond the page: the drop tables are in the game's code, which anyone may read.
- **Decided.** A drop shows twice, and only when the party got it (decision 0058; the scene's part built by Issue-344). While an adventure is played, the party panel's "Carrying" line counts up the moment an enemy falls and something drops. In the scene, the resource's icon rises from the fallen enemy as it falls, with the amount on its lower right in pixel digits, the scene's first text, and is gone; the adventure's clock waits for the rise as it waits for the fall, for the longer of the two (decision 0057). Neither says anything for a drop the party could not gather, so the scene never shows a drop that was missed or hints at one. A replay shows the same, from the stored events. Where a fight follows a gather at once, the last node's drop rises as the road carries it off behind the party. **Working assumption,** set by looking: the icon rises 14 px in 0.6 seconds, quickly at first, and fades over the last of it.
- **Decided.** Enemies give XP, and a retreat keeps what was won (decision 0058; built by Issue-339). Each enemy, a node included, has XP; a hero that survives earns the XP of every enemy that went down, in full, and on arrival the job's bonus. A retreat keeps the drops and the enemies' XP and loses the pay and the bonus. A party that is all down keeps nothing. **Working assumption,** the numbers: a tree and an ore vein give 3 XP, a wolf and a goblin rock thrower 5, a bandit 6, and the bonus of each of the five jobs that had XP before the enemies did is what brings its XP to what it gave then, counting the enemies that always appear; the lumber camp's job, written since, has a bonus of 6, which brings its least roll to the ore job's 15, and Highcrag's board job has the ore job's stages, its bonus of 4 and its 15 (decision 0059); the caravan hub's scouting job has a bonus of 8, which brings its two bandits' 12 to the wolves job's 20 (decision 0059); an enemy that appears by chance adds its own.
- **Decided.** A job's adventures vary (decision 0058; built by Issue-341). Each entry of a fight or a gather stage has a count or a range for it, and may have a chance of appearing at all, so the adventures of a job have the same stages and about the same enemies, from a limited pool, and are not the same: gathering sticks is usually a tree, and once in a while a wolf. The roll is made from the adventure's seed, which is issued after the party is picked, so the player picks without seeing it. A job may have no variance. Every fight and gather stage has something that always appears, its time limit fits its largest roll, and that roll brings home no more of a resource than a level-1 warehouse holds, unless the owner has accepted more for the job. The lumber camp's job is the one that has (decision 0058): the board's timber job already reaches a level-1 warehouse's 100 at a lumberjack's best roll, so a job with clearly more timber passes it, and this one's timber is held to a level-2 warehouse's 200, its every other resource to a level-1 warehouse like any job's. A camp that has not built the warehouse's second level loses what is over, and the result says how much; the job's page gives no warning, since the room for a drop would name it. **Working assumption:** "Pick up sticks" meets a wolf one time in ten; "Lumber from the deep woods" is three trees or four, the first count that is a range, with a wolf or two on half its runs. They are the two jobs that vary so far.
