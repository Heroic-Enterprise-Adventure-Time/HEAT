# The checkup: reviewing the whole tree at a pause

How the tree on `main` is reviewed against the standards at a pause, a checkpoint merged or a step of the build order begun (`docs/process/best-practices.md`, rule 9): a model-driven review, run from a session of its own, over the whole tree rather than a diff, whose findings are issues the owner triages (`docs/process/code-changes.md`, rule 28). It is the third layer of decision 0033, above lint and the review skill (`docs/runbooks/review-change.md`), and reports nothing those two catch. The Claude Code skill `.claude/skills/checkup/` points here, decision 0034 records why a finding is an issue, and decision 0049 why findings are filed in batches. The rules below are the procedure; the brief the session works from is at the end.

## Rules

1. **When, and by whom.** At a pause, in a session started for the checkup and nothing else, never one with an issue in flight. The owner starts it, or starts it from the card a session proposes at the pause; the session is given the brief below and the background the app requires, and no steps of its own. It runs over the whole tree, or over one area of rule 4's table when the owner names one: after a triage, to pick up the batches the cap of rule 7 left, or when a step of the build order touched one area.
2. **The tree is `main`, read in place.** The session reads the checkout, which must be a clean `main` at `origin/main` after `git fetch origin` (`git status --porcelain` prints nothing and `git rev-parse HEAD` equals `git rev-parse origin/main`); otherwise it stops and says so, and does not stash, reset or switch. It makes no worktree and changes no file. Its only writes are the batch and finding issues of rule 8, the accepted rules that rule 10 adds to a batch and, where they are missing, the labels of rule 9. It reports the commit it reviewed.
3. **Lint has run.** The latest "Build and test" run on `main` is green (`gh run list --branch main --workflow "Build and test" --limit 1`); where it is red or still running, the session says so and stops. The checkup reports nothing a lint rule or tflint expresses (`docs/standards/typescript.md`, rule 16; `docs/standards/terraform.md`, rule 17).
4. **One pass per area.** Each area is reviewed in a pass of its own, with only that area's documents and files in view, so that a pass reads one rubric closely rather than every rubric thinly; the passes may run beside each other. A pass reads its documents in full before any file, then every file under its paths, and reports its findings in the format of rule 6 to the session, which reads the cited lines itself before it files anything (rule 8). The brief a pass is given is the brief at the end of this document, with the area filled in.

   | Area | Paths | Documents |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Packages | `packages/engine/**`, `packages/AGENTS.md`, `scripts/**` but its `.sh` files, `manifest.json`, `package.json`, `tsconfig*.json`, `eslint.config.mjs`, `.node-version` | `docs/standards/typescript.md` |
   | Client | `packages/client/**` | `docs/standards/angular.md` and `docs/standards/typescript.md` |
   | Hub | `packages/hub/**` | `docs/standards/graphql.md` and `docs/standards/typescript.md` |
   | Terraform | `terraform/**` | `docs/standards/terraform.md` |
   | Pipeline | `.github/**`, `scripts/**/*.sh`, `.claude/settings.json`, `terraform/allowed-resource-types.txt` | `docs/process/code-changes.md` and `docs/process/best-practices.md`, which hold the guardrails |
   | Documents | `docs/**`, `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, every `AGENTS.md` and `CLAUDE.md` under an area, `.claude/skills/**` | `docs/README.md`; `docs/decisions/README.md` for `docs/decisions/**` |
   | Art | `art/**` | `docs/process/assets.md` |

   The Packages pass reads the engine and what is common to every package; the Client and Hub passes read their package under both their documents and report against either, so that no file is read by two passes. A pass reads nothing outside its paths and documents, except a document or decision record that one of its rules names by number, and `eslint.config.mjs` or `terraform/.tflint.hcl`, which say what lint already expresses.
5. **The documents pass does the sweep, and asks the drift questions.** Beside its rubric it does what `docs/README.md` rule 8 names for a pause: history that has crept into a design or process document is a finding under that rule, and a document that describes as built, applied or enforced what is not is one under rule 6. Every item under a standards document's "Held back" is checked: its issue exists and is open, or the item is stale (rule 5 of `docs/README.md`). An item that states a release condition has that condition tested against the registry, and a condition that has been met is a finding, cited under the rule that holds the item back. Then, for each standards document, the two questions of `docs/process/best-practices.md`, rule 9: does the shape still fit the requirements, and has current guidance moved from what the document states as practice. A claim that guidance has moved is checked against the provider's or the framework's documentation, not recalled, and names the page read and the date (best-practices, rule 3). A drift that breaches a numbered rule, a major not taken under `typescript.md` rule 1, a practice stated and not followed, is a finding; one that breaches none is raised in the session as rule 10 says, never filed.
6. **A finding cites one rule.** Each finding is one breach of one numbered rule of one of the documents in rule 4's table: the severity, the document and rule number, the files with their lines, what breaches the rule, and a proposed shape of the fix, in a few lines. The severities are those of the review skill: **blocks**, a breach of a rule that guards a guardrail, security, a secret, money, or the game's data and state (the tables, the ledger, the replays, Terraform state; not the project's own files, whose provenance and documents are at most **fix**); **fix**, any other rule breached; and **note**, a breach with no effect beyond the reader. A rule a document lists under "Held back" is not breached by code it names as held back. An ambiguous rule is read as its document's "Why" section means it, or as written where the document has none; where it is still ambiguous, nothing is reported. A claim of enforcement that is not true, in a section without numbered rules such as `docs/process/code-changes.md`, "What enforces this", is cited under `docs/README.md` rule 6. A **note** is never filed on its own: it is listed in a batch whose pull request touches its file anyway, one filed in this run or an open one from an earlier run, whose body the session rewrites to add it, under "Notes: fix only where the pull request touches the file anyway" (rule 8), and otherwise it is dropped. Zero findings is a checkup, not a failure.
7. **Deduplicated, then capped.** Before anything is filed the session reads every issue labeled `finding`, open and closed (`gh issue list --label finding --state all --limit 1000 --json number,title,state,labels,body`; where the count returned equals the limit, the session raises the limit and reads again, because an excused finding the list cut off would be filed anew). The same rule on the same file as an open `finding` is already filed and is skipped; as a `finding` closed with the label `excused` it is excused and is skipped, however long ago; as a `finding` closed without that label it was fixed and has come back, so it is filed anew and its body names the old issue. The session then reads the body of every open batch issue of rule 8 (`gh issue list --state open --search '"From the checkup of" in:body -label:finding' --limit 100 --json number,title,body`), and an item listed in one is skipped while that batch is open. At most six batches are filed per run, each half of a split batch counting as one, and a batch's size is bounded by rule 8's review line by line, not by a count; the most serious are filed first, a batch being as serious as its most serious finding, and, within a severity, one area's batch in turn in the order of rule 4's table, so that a whole-tree run shows every area; what the cap leaves is counted per area and severity in the session's final message, and a later run of that area picks it up.
8. **Findings are filed in batches, a finding is an issue.** The verified findings are grouped by the pull request that would fix them, one batch issue per group; findings whose milestones differ go in different batches, and a batch too large to review line by line (`docs/process/code-changes.md`, rule 7) is split in two. Every body is written to a file first and passed with `--body-file`.
   - **The batch** is created first, with `gh issue create --title <title> --body-file <file> --milestone <milestone>`. Its title names the area and what the pull request makes true, under about 80 characters. Its body opens with a paragraph naming the documents the batch brings the code to and the paths of its one pull request, then has these sections, each only where it has items: "Findings (sub-issues, closed by the pull request)", a task item per finding issue with its number and one line; "Rules the owner accepted" (rule 10); "Also in scope (reported by the checkup's pass; check the lines before fixing)", what a pass reported and the session has not read line by line, with the document and rule it cites, or none where rule 10 gave it no rule; and "Notes: fix only where the pull request touches the file anyway" (rule 6). It ends with the line `From the checkup of <short commit> on main, <date>.` and the closing line below. Once its findings are filed, the body is rewritten with their numbers (`gh issue edit <batch> --body-file <file>`).
   - **Each verified finding** is one issue, a sub-issue of its batch, created with `gh issue create --title <title> --body-file <file> --label finding --milestone <milestone> --parent <batch>`, so that the deduplication of rule 7 still reads one breach per issue. The title says what is wrong and where, in plain words, under about 80 characters, with no severity or rule in it. The body opens with the line `**Checkup · <severity> · <document>, rule N**`, then the files and lines, the breach, the proposed shape of the fix, and the line `Reviewed <short commit> on main, <date>.`.
   - **In both,** the date is the owner's local one, and the body ends, after a blank line, with the closing line `_🤖 Filed by the checkup skill ([Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code))_`, because the issue is created under the owner's login and a reader must be able to tell. The milestone is "1 Platform", the housekeeping milestone, unless the fix changes what the player sees, when it is the milestone of the build-order step that owns that feature (`docs/architecture/overview.md`, "Build order"); a finding takes its batch's milestone. No assignee, and no label but `finding` on a finding. The batch's pull request closes the batch and each finding, with one `Closes #n` per issue.
9. **Two labels, with descriptions.** `finding`: "Checkup finding: one breach of one rule. Open is accepted; closed with excused is not raised again." `excused`: "Closed by the owner with a one-line reason; the checkup does not raise it again." A description is at most 100 characters, GitHub's limit. Created once, by the pull request that introduced this runbook; where either is missing, the session creates it with that description (`gh label create <name> --description <text>`) before filing, and says so.
10. **Candidate rules go to the owner, in the session, and few earn one.** What a pass would report but no numbered rule covers is not a finding. A candidate becomes a rule only when it does at least one of three things: it guards security, money or the game's data; it is enforced by a machine, the compiler, lint or a test, so that keeping it costs nothing after the first fix; or it loosens a rule that already produces false findings. Anything else has its instances listed in a batch under "Also in scope" (rule 8) and no rule. The session's final message lists each candidate that passes the test as a rule worded for the document it would join, with the part of the test it passes and the place that prompted it, and asks the owner; it names the others in a line each, with the batch their instances went to. Before the session ends, an accepted rule is added, with its instances, to the batch whose pull request touches its document (`docs/process/best-practices.md`, rule 12), under "Rules the owner accepted", since chat is the one surface nothing reads back (`docs/process/collaboration.md`, "In practice"); where no batch touches that document, the rule is a batch of its own under "1 Platform", outside the cap. A rejected one is dropped. Where the owner is not in the session, a side session reporting to another, the list goes in its final message and its hand-over note, and the owner adds an accepted one to its batch or hands it to a session.
11. **The owner triages on GitHub,** as `docs/process/code-changes.md`, rule 28 says. The session that ran the checkup fixes nothing and decides nothing.

## The session's brief

What the checkup session is told when it starts, with the area filled in where the owner names one. Nothing is added to it.

```text
Run the checkup of the HEAT repository, following docs/runbooks/checkup.md on main: review the whole tree (or only the area <area>) against the numbered rules of the documents the runbook's table maps to each area, from a clean checkout at origin/main. Read every finding issue, open and closed, before filing anything. Report only a breach of a numbered rule, cited by document and number, with the file and lines; what no rule covers is a candidate rule, weighed by the runbook's rule 10 and put to the owner in your final message. Deduplicate, cap and file in batches as the runbook's rules 7 and 8 say. Change no file, make no worktree, push nothing, fix nothing. Read an ambiguous rule as its document's "Why" section means it, or as written where it has none; where it is still ambiguous, do not report. A rule a document lists under "Held back" is not breached by code it names as held back.
```

The brief a pass is given adds its row of the table, the finding format of rule 6, and rule 4's instruction on what it reads and what it may read beyond that.

## Why

The checkup reviews the tree in place rather than a diff because the review skill already reads every change as it lands and lint already fails what it can express; what neither sees is the shape that forms across changes, a rule added after the code it would have governed, a document that drifted from the code, a hold-back whose issue closed. Those are visible only in the whole tree, and only at a pause, when nothing is in flight to confuse the reading.

A finding is an issue because the open issues are the backlog and there is no other list (`docs/process/code-changes.md`, rule 1): what is accepted has to become a checkpoint, and the batch already is one, with each finding under it an issue the owner can excuse on its own. The closed issue with `excused` is the suppression record for the same reason in reverse: the decision not to fix something is made where the finding was made, by the owner, with a reason, on a surface a later session reads cold and a phone shows. A file of suppressions in the tree would be a second list, and a report comment would be read once (decision 0034).

One pass per area, each with one rubric, because a reader given every rule reads each thinly, and because the rubrics were written one per area for the same reason. Findings are filed in batches, one per pull request that fixes them, because the unit of work is the pull request: one issue per breach is a grain too small to triage or to work from, and the owner regrouped the ten findings of 2026-11-09 by hand into six batches (Issue-273, decision 0049). Each finding stays an issue of its own under its batch, so that the deduplication still reads one breach per issue and an excuse still closes one breach. The cap counts batches because the owner triages and schedules pull requests, not breaches, and because a run on the subscription is bounded by its usage limits (decision 0033).

Few candidates earn a rule because a rule that only review enforces is broken by every iteration and refiled by every checkup, which spends the work on adherence rather than on the game. A rule that guards what cannot be got back, security, money and the game's data, earns its cost; a rule a machine checks costs nothing to keep; a rule loosened stops false findings. Everything else is fixed where it is found. The session reads each cited line before filing because a pass reports in the same confident register whether it is right or wrong, and an issue is a durable thing.
